# Final Introduction

The final moves from how cells work to **how engineers use biology**: copying and editing DNA, the immune system and vaccines, the nervous system and brain-computer interfaces, computing on biological data, and designing biological systems. It is a separate syllabus from the midterm; the midterm chapters are not repeated in the final suggestion.

## Final scope

The supplied final suggestion has five chapters, one per final lecture.

| # | Chapter | What to know |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Biotechnology** | PCR (steps, diagram, graph), gel electrophoresis, DNA fingerprinting, recombinant DNA technology (steps, diagram), bacterial competency |
| 2 | **Immunity** | Innate versus adaptive immunity, NK cells, vaccination |
| 3 | **Brain-computer interface** | Sensory and motor neurons, the action potential (steps and graph), the synapse, BCI parts and applications, artificial neural networks |
| 4 | **Biological databases** | Database definition and classification, BLAST (E-value, query coverage, identity), UniProt versus PDB, phylogenetic trees |
| 5 | **Systems and synthetic biology** | Gene regulation by activators and repressors, feedback loops, the DBTL cycle, gene-editing tools (CRISPR-Cas9, BioBricks) |

## The final paper

- **40 marks, 2 hours.**
- **Fall 2025 and Spring 2026:** five questions; 1, 2 and 3 are compulsory, answer **either 4 or 5**.
- **Summer 2026:** six questions, answer **any five**.
- Parts carry 2 to 5 marks, each with a course outcome (CO1 to CO4).

The **Summer 2025** final (in the question bank) followed an older syllabus, with questions on food webs, diet and stress management that are not in the current suggestion. The Fall 2025, Spring 2026 and Summer 2026 finals match the suggestion closely: BCI components, innate versus adaptive immunity, NK cells, vaccines, DBTL, feedback loops, databases and recombinant DNA appear again and again.

## How this section is organised

1. **Introduction** (this page): scope and exam format.
2. **Lecture Slides:** a guide to the five final lecture decks.
3. **Main Topics:** the key concepts of each chapter.
4. **Original Slide Figures:** the slides themselves, grouped by chapter.
5. **Suggestions:** the supplied final suggestion.
6. **Solutions:** answers matched to the suggestion items, with the original slides beside each one.

> **Where the solutions come from.** The supplied final solutions (a typed answer set of 44 answers with figures) cover most suggestion items. They were checked against the slides; a few factual slips are corrected and each correction is labelled. Items with no supplied answer (for example phylogenetic tree construction, BioBricks and the PCR graph) were answered from the lecture slides.
