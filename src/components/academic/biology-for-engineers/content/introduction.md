# General Introduction

**Biology for Engineers (BIO 3105)** is a core 3-credit course for computer science and engineering students at United International University. It explains how living systems work and how engineers use that knowledge, from reading DNA as data to building devices that talk to the brain.

::fig intro-biology-and-computing | Biology and computing meet in modern engineering | Lecture 1–2, slide 1

## Why an engineer studies biology

Biology has become one of the largest sources of data in the world. Every DNA sequence, medical image, protein structure and brain signal is data, and analysing data is what computer scientists and data scientists do. The opening lectures make the point with examples you will meet again in the exams:

- **AI in biology:** the 2024 Nobel Prize in Chemistry went to computer scientists and a biochemist for predicting and designing protein structures with AI (AlphaFold).
- **Diagnosis from images:** machine learning separates harmless moles from melanoma in skin images, and explainable-AI heatmaps show which regions drove the decision.
- **Biomedical devices:** prosthetic limbs, retinal implants, pacemakers and cochlear implants must work with living tissue.
- **Drug delivery:** nanoparticles carry drugs to tumours and reduce side effects.
- **Biosensors and lab-on-a-chip devices** detect diseases early and at low cost.
- **Biomimicry:** Velcro copies the hooks of burdock burrs; shark skin inspires drag-reducing surfaces.

## What the course covers

The official course outline lists eight content areas. The course is taught in two halves, and these notes follow the same split.

| Part | Content (from the course outline) | Exam |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Introduction:** the basics of life and why biology matters to engineers | Midterm |
| 2 | **Cell structure and cell division:** cell structure and function, mitosis, meiosis, membrane transport, the fluid mosaic model | Midterm |
| 3 | **Genetics and genomics:** DNA and RNA structure and replication, the central dogma, Mendel's laws, genes and chromosomes, mutations and genetic disorders | Midterm |
| 4 | **Molecular biology and biotechnology:** PCR, gel electrophoresis, recombinant DNA, CRISPR and gene editing, personalised medicine | Final |
| 5 | **Immunity:** innate and adaptive immunity, their components, vaccination | Final |
| 6 | **Structural and systems biology:** biomolecules and protein structure, biological networks, omics, synthetic biology | Midterm (structure) and final (systems) |
| 7 | **Biological data and databases:** GenBank, BLAST, PDB, UniProt, phylogenetic trees | Final |
| 8 | **Brain-computer interface:** neurons, action potentials, synapses, BCI and artificial neural networks | Final |

The textbook is *Biology for Engineers* by Arthur T. Johnson (2nd edition), with *Biology for Engineers* by G. K. Suraishkumar as the second reference used on the slides.

## How these notes are organised

1. **This introduction.**
2. **Midterm preparation:** the midterm scope, the lecture slides, the main topics, the original slide figures, the suggestion list and the solutions.
3. **Final preparation:** the same six pages for the final.
4. **Question bank:** nine previous midterm and final papers, questions only.

The suggestions and their solutions are the heart of each exam section. Every solution shows the original slide figure that explains it, so you revise the answer and the diagram together.

> **Labels on the solutions.** *Supplied solution* means the answer comes from the solutions provided for this course (checked against the slides). *Corrected* means the supplied text had a mistake, and the answer says what was changed. *Prepared from the slides* means no solution was supplied, so the answer was written from the lecture slides. *No slide in the materials* marks the few answers where the supplied slides do not cover the topic; check those against your class notes.
