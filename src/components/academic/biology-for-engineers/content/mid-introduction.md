# Midterm Introduction

The midterm covers the first half of the course: **how a cell is built, how it divides, and how it stores and passes on genetic information**, plus the introduction lectures on why biology matters to engineers.

## Midterm scope

The supplied midterm suggestion groups the syllabus into five chapters. These notes use the same five chapters everywhere in the midterm section.

| # | Chapter | What to know |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Introduction** | Ecology (biocenosis, biosphere, ecosystem), evolution, why engineers study biology, biosensors, self-healing concrete, biopesticides, organ-on-a-chip |
| 2 | **Cell structure** | Cell theory, the fluid mosaic model, active and passive transport, Golgi body and exocytosis, endoplasmic reticulum, lysosomes and autolysosomes |
| 3 | **Cell division** | Haploid and diploid, prophase of mitosis versus prophase I of meiosis, the cell cycle and its checkpoints, external and internal regulators, genetic variation |
| 4 | **Genetics and genomics** | Central dogma, DNA replication, DNA versus RNA, Mendel's principles, chromosomal abnormalities |
| 5 | **Structural biology** | Biomolecules and their monomers, the four levels of protein structure, Cryo-EM and NMR |

## The midterm paper

All five previous midterm papers in the question bank have the same shape:

- **30 marks, 1 hour 30 minutes.**
- **Five questions.** Questions 1, 2 and 3 are compulsory; answer **either 4 or 5**.
- Each part carries 1 to 4 marks and a course outcome (CO1 to CO4).

Questions repeat from paper to paper. The most frequent ones are the **prophase of mitosis versus prophase I** (four papers), **the cell theory**, **Golgi body and exocytosis**, **DNA versus RNA as genetic material**, **the cell cycle with checkpoints**, **genetic variation in meiosis** and an **"apply your CSE knowledge to biology"** question. All of them are in the suggestions.

## How this section is organised

1. **Introduction** (this page): the scope and the exam format.
2. **Lecture Slides:** a short guide to each of the five midterm lecture decks.

5. **Suggestions:** the supplied suggestion list, organised by chapter.
6. **Solutions:** an answer to every suggested item, with the original slide figure next to it.

> **Where the solutions come from.** The supplied midterm solutions are a handwritten answer set of 20 answers. They were transcribed, checked against the slides, and corrected or extended where needed. Suggested items without a supplied answer were answered from the lecture slides. The midterm decks do not cover the central dogma, DNA replication, Mendel's laws or protein-structure methods; those answers are marked *No slide in the materials*.
