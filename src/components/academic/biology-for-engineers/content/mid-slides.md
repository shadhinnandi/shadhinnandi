# Midterm Lecture Slides

Five lecture decks make up the midterm material. Each section below says what the deck covers, which suggestion chapter it serves and which slides matter most for the exam.

## Lectures 1–2: Introduction to Biology for Engineers

41 slides. **Chapter 1 (Introduction).**

- **Slides 3–17: why biology in engineering.** Biology as a data source, AI in diagnosis (melanoma detection and explainable AI), prosthetics, nano drug delivery, biosensors, brain and neuroscience, and biomimicry (Velcro, shark skin).
- **Slides 18–22: exploring the living world.** The cell as the smallest unit of life; definitions of **biocenosis, biosphere and ecosystem**; reproduction and genetics; **ecosystems and evolution**.
- **Slides 23–41: scope for engineers.** **Organ-on-a-chip**, tissue engineering, scaffolds, artificial neural networks, bio-robotics, nanoparticles, bioinformatics, the retinal prosthetic, **biosensors**, biochips, **biopesticides**, **self-healing concrete** and biofilters.

::fig intro-levels-of-organisation | Levels of organisation from atom to biosphere, with biocenosis and ecosystem | Lecture 1–2, slide 18

## Lecture 3: Cell Structure and Function

33 slides. **Chapter 2 (Cell structure).**

- **Slides 3–5: cell theory.** History (Hooke 1665, Leeuwenhoek 1674, Schleiden, Schwann, Virchow) and the **modern cell theory**.
- **Slides 6–14: cell types.** Prokaryotic versus eukaryotic cells, the labelled prokaryotic cell (capsule, cell wall, pili, flagellum, nucleoid, ribosomes), plant versus animal cells.
- **Slides 15–25: the cell membrane.** Its lipids (phospholipids, glycolipids, cholesterol) and proteins (integral, peripheral, glycoproteins), and the **fluid mosaic model** of Singer and Nicolson (1974).
- **Slides 27–33: transport across the membrane.** Passive transport (diffusion, osmosis, facilitated diffusion), tonicity, active transport with protein pumps, endocytosis and exocytosis.

::fig cell-fluid-mosaic-model | The fluid mosaic model of the plasma membrane, the key diagram of this lecture | Lecture 3, slide 24

## Lecture 4: Cell Structure and Function (organelles)

41 slides. **Chapter 2 (Cell structure)**, with cell-cycle regulators for Chapter 3.

- **Slides 2–7: protoplasm, cytoplasm and an organelle summary table.**
- **Slides 8–13: membrane organelles.** **Endoplasmic reticulum** (rough and smooth), **Golgi body** ("packaging centre"), with the **exocytosis** diagram on slide 11, and mitochondria.
- **Slides 14–24: plastids, vacuoles, microbodies** (lysosomes, **autolysosome formation** on slide 20, peroxisomes, glyoxysomes), ribosomes and the centrosome.
- **Slides 26–33: nucleus and chromosomes**, from DNA to the metaphase chromosome.
- **Slides 39–41: cell-cycle regulators** (cyclin, internal and external regulators) and cancer.

::fig organelles-golgi-exocytosis | Golgi apparatus and exocytosis: constitutive and regulated secretion | Lecture 4, slide 11

## Cell Division and Inheritance

55 slides. **Chapter 3 (Cell division).**

- **Slides 2–11: why cells divide**, chromosomes and DNA, chromosome numbers by species.
- **Slides 12–20: the cell cycle.** Interphase (G1, S, G2) and the four phases of mitosis (PMAT).
- **Slides 21–29: control of the cell cycle.** External positive and negative signals, the three internal checkpoints.
- **Slides 30–33: cancer.** Proto-oncogenes and tumour suppressor genes.
- **Slides 34–52: meiosis.** Haploid and diploid, **prophase I** (synapsis, crossing over, chiasmata, tetrads and its five substages), metaphase I to telophase II.
- **Slides 53–55: genetic variation in meiosis.** **Crossing over** and **random orientation**.

::fig division-cell-cycle | The cell cycle: interphase (G1, S, G2) and the mitotic phase | Cell Division, slide 13

## Lecture 7: Genetics and Genomics

13 slides (also supplied as a PDF). **Chapter 4 (Genetics and genomics).**

- **Slides 3–6: nucleotides.** Three parts (phosphate, pentose sugar, nitrogenous base); purines and pyrimidines; ribose versus deoxyribose.
- **Slides 7–10: DNA.** The double helix (Watson and Crick), complementary base pairing and **antiparallel strands**.
- **Slides 11–13: genes.** What a gene is, alleles, loci and the human gene count.

::fig genetics-nucleotide-parts | The three parts of a nucleotide | Lecture 7, slide 4
