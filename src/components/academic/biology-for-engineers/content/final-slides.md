# Final Lecture Slides

The five final lectures were supplied as PDFs, so the figures in this section are the **whole original slides**, rendered from those files. Each deck below lists what it covers and the slides that matter most for the suggestion.

## Lecture 6: Biotechnology

65 slides. **Chapter 1 (Biotechnology).**

- **Slides 1–10: what biotechnology is**, its applications (mRNA vaccines, CAR-T cells, insulin, crops), the "rainbow" colours of biotechnology and the product workflow.
- **Slides 14–24: PCR.** The "DNA photocopier": master mix and Taq polymerase, the components (primers, polymerase, nucleotides, buffer), the three steps (denaturation, annealing, extension) and the temperature-time graph on slide 22.
- **Slides 25–29: gel electrophoresis and DNA fingerprinting (RFLP).**
- **Slides 30–43: recombinant DNA technology.** The six cloning steps, DNA isolation and its enzymes, restriction digestion, PCR amplification, ligation, transformation with a selectable marker, host cells, bioreactors and downstream processing.
- **Slides 44–51: tools of biotechnology.** Restriction enzymes (EcoRI), cloning vectors (ori, selectable marker, cloning site) and competent hosts.
- **Slides 52–65: applications.** Insulin, gene therapy, pharmacogenomics, GM crops, transgenic animals and ethical issues.

::fig bt-pcr-steps | Steps in a PCR reaction: denaturing, annealing and extending | Lecture 6, slide 18

## Lecture 7: Immunity and the Immune System

40 slides. **Chapter 2 (Immunity).**

- **Slides 2–11: the immune system**, its organs (thymus, spleen, lymph nodes, bone marrow), innate versus adaptive immunity and the lymphatic system.
- **Slides 12–21: white blood cells**, T-cell types, antibodies (five classes), monoclonal and polyclonal antibodies, antigens, the complement system, **NK cells** and mast cells.
- **Slides 24–28: immune disorders** and how innate and adaptive responses fight a pathogen.
- **Slides 29–40: vaccination.** The HALO considerations, how vaccines work, herd immunity, the primary and secondary response curve, vaccine types and their benefits and risks.

::fig im-innate-vs-adaptive | Innate versus adaptive immunity | Lecture 7, slide 7

## Lecture 8: Brain-Computer Interface

33 slides. **Chapter 3 (Brain-computer interface).**

- **Slides 2–7: neuroscience**, the major parts and lobes of the brain, glial cells.
- **Slides 8–15: neurons.** Structure (cell body, axon, dendrites), classification by poles and by function, sensory (afferent) and motor (efferent) neurons.
- **Slides 16–21: signalling.** The synapse, the ten-step sequence at a chemical synapse, neurotransmitters versus neuropeptides, the Na⁺/K⁺ pump and the **action potential**.
- **Slides 22–29: neuroengineering and BCI.** What a BCI is, its three parts, EEG, and who can use BCIs.
- **Slides 30–33: artificial neural networks** and how they compare with biological ones.

::fig bci-action-potential | The action potential | Lecture 8, slide 21

## Lecture 9: Biological Databases

40 slides. **Chapter 4 (Biological databases).**

- **Slides 2–5: databases.** Definition, the three categories (primary, secondary, specialised) and the omics.
- **Slides 6–16: NCBI, GenBank and BLAST.** Accession numbers, FASTA files, the five types of BLAST and the BLAST outcomes (max score, total score, query coverage, identity, E-value).
- **Slides 17–22: protein databases.** PDB, UniProt, their differences, protein structure levels, SWISS-Model and AlphaFold 3.
- **Slides 23–28: applications** and the Galaxy server for genome analysis.
- **Slides 29–39: phylogenetic trees.** Construction with 16S rRNA, terminology, types (cladogram, phylogram, ultrametric) and applications.

::fig db-uniprot-vs-pdb | Difference between UniProt and PDB | Lecture 9, slide 19

## Lecture 10: Systems Biology

32 slides. **Chapter 5 (Systems and synthetic biology).**

- **Slides 2–14: systems biology.** From parts to systems, biological systems as networks of nodes and edges, metabolic, gene regulatory and signalling networks, **activators and repressors** (slide 12).
- **Slides 15–19: properties of complex systems.** Nonlinearity, **feedback loops** and robustness.
- **Slides 20–27: modelling and omics.**
- **Slides 28–32: synthetic biology.** Definition, the **DBTL cycle**, DNA synthesis, **BioBricks**, **CRISPR-Cas9** and applications.
