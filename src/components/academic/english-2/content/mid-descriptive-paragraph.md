# Descriptive Paragraph

## What it is

The slides define it: *a descriptive paragraph describes a person, place, object, event, or experience by using vivid sensory details so that the reader can clearly imagine it.* Its purpose is to make readers **see, feel and hear** what you have seen, felt and heard.

Features from the slides:

- Focuses on **one subject**.
- Uses **sensory details**: sight, sound, smell, taste, touch.
- Contains many **adjectives and descriptive words**.
- Creates a **clear mental picture**.
- Usually follows **spatial order** (left to right, top to bottom, near to far) or **order of importance**.
- Mostly written in the **present tense**, unless you describe something in the past.

## The structure

| Part | What to write |
|---|---|
| Topic sentence | The subject + your **dominant impression** (the one feeling the whole paragraph creates: *bright and welcoming*, *calm*, *chaotic*) |
| Supporting point 1 | First feature, with sensory details and an example |
| Supporting point 2 | Second feature, with sensory details and an example |
| Supporting point 3 | Third feature, with sensory details and an example |
| Concluding sentence | Restate the impression in new words |

### The slide example, analysed

The slides describe **My Classroom**:

| Part | Text |
|---|---|
| Topic sentence | My classroom is a bright and welcoming place where I enjoy learning every day. |
| Supporting detail 1 | First of all, the walls are painted light blue and decorated with colorful charts, maps, and students' artwork… For example, the grammar charts and maps are useful whenever we need quick references during lessons. |
| Supporting detail 2 | Moreover, large windows allow plenty of natural sunlight to enter the room. As a result, the classroom remains bright and fresh… |
| Supporting detail 3 | Finally, the desks are neatly arranged, and the room is always clean and organized… |
| Concluding sentence | To conclude, my classroom provides a comfortable and inspiring environment for learning. |

Every point supports the dominant impression *bright and welcoming*, and each one moves from a detail (light blue walls) to its effect (lively) to an example (grammar charts).

## Step-by-step method

1. **Choose the dominant impression.** Ask: *What one feeling do I want the reader to have?* Put it in the topic sentence.
2. **Collect details with the five senses.** Make a quick table: what do you see, hear, smell, touch, taste?
3. **Pick three features** that best create the impression and decide the order: spatial (entrance → middle → back) or importance (least → most striking).
4. **Show, don't tell.** Replace general words with specific ones: not *"the food was nice"* but *"the smell of frying onions and cumin filled the room"*.
5. **Write the outline**, then the paragraph with transitions (*First of all, Moreover, Finally, To conclude*).
6. **Check tense:** present for a place or person as they are now; past for a scene you remember.

### Showing versus telling

| Telling (weak) | Showing (strong) |
|---|---|
| The market was noisy. | Sellers shouted their prices over the honking of rickshaw bells. |
| The room was old. | Paint peeled from the walls, and the wooden floor creaked under every step. |
| She looked happy. | Her eyes shone, and she laughed at the smallest joke. |

## Revision summary

> **Topic sentence** with a dominant impression → **three features** in spatial order or order of importance → each with **sensory details** + example → **concluding sentence** restating the impression.
>
> Senses: sight, sound, smell, touch, taste. Show, don't tell. Present tense unless it is a memory.

## Model paragraphs

Four models on different kinds of subject: a place, a busy public scene, a person and a season. Each follows the slides' format. Study how the outline turns into the paragraph.

:::model Model paragraph 1 · A place | My Grandmother's Kitchen
--- outline
- **Topic:** My grandmother's kitchen
- **Topic sentence:** My grandmother's kitchen is the warmest and most welcoming room in our village home.
- **Supporting point 1:** The smells of cooking
- **Supporting point 2:** The old, familiar objects
- **Supporting point 3:** The family gathering around the clay stove
- **Concluding sentence:** To conclude, my grandmother's kitchen is not just a place to cook but the heart of our family.
--- answer Paragraph
My grandmother's kitchen is the warmest and most welcoming room in our village home. First of all, the kitchen is always full of rich, comforting smells. From early morning, the scent of boiling rice mixes with the sharp smell of mustard oil and frying onions. For example, when she prepares hilsa curry, the aroma of turmeric and green chillies reaches the courtyard and draws everyone inside. Moreover, the room is filled with old, familiar objects. Brass pots shine on a wooden shelf, a stone grinder rests in one corner, and bunches of dried red chillies hang from the bamboo ceiling. These simple things have been used for decades, so they give the kitchen a feeling of history. Finally, the kitchen is where the whole family gathers. In the evening, we sit on low wooden stools around the clay stove, feel the heat of the fire on our faces and listen to my grandmother's stories while the rice cooks. To conclude, my grandmother's kitchen is not just a place to cook but the heart of our family.
--- note
The dominant impression (*warmest and most welcoming*) controls every point. Point 1 uses **smell**, point 2 **sight**, point 3 **touch and sound**, so the paragraph covers several senses. Transitions follow the slides: *First of all, Moreover, Finally, To conclude*.
:::

:::model Model paragraph 2 · A busy scene | A Railway Station at Rush Hour
--- outline
- **Topic:** A railway station at rush hour
- **Topic sentence:** Kamalapur Railway Station at rush hour is a crowded, noisy and colourful scene.
- **Supporting point 1:** The crowd on the platform
- **Supporting point 2:** The sounds of the station
- **Supporting point 3:** The vendors and their goods
- **Concluding sentence:** In short, the station at rush hour is a lively picture of the energy of city life.
--- answer Paragraph
Kamalapur Railway Station at rush hour is a crowded, noisy and colourful scene. Firstly, the platforms are packed with people. Office workers in pressed shirts walk quickly with their bags, families sit on large bundles waiting for their train, and porters in red uniforms carry heavy suitcases on their heads through the crowd. For example, when a train arrives, hundreds of passengers rush towards the doors at the same moment. Secondly, the station is full of sound. A voice on the loudspeaker announces arrivals and departures, train engines roar and whistle, and people shout to find their friends. In fact, it is almost impossible to hold a quiet conversation. Finally, vendors add colour and smell to the scene. Tea sellers pour steaming cups from large kettles, and boys walk along the platform selling newspapers, roasted peanuts and bright plastic toys. The smell of fried snacks mixes with the smell of diesel in the warm air. In short, the station at rush hour is a lively picture of the energy of city life.
--- note
A scene full of movement is described in **order of impression**: what you see first (crowd), what you hear (sounds), then the smaller details (vendors). Specific nouns (*porters in red uniforms*, *roasted peanuts*) make the reader see the station instead of being told it is busy.
:::

:::model Model paragraph 3 · A person | My Favourite Teacher
--- outline
- **Topic:** My favourite teacher
- **Topic sentence:** My mathematics teacher in secondary school, Mr. Rahman, was the kindest and most inspiring teacher I have known.
- **Supporting point 1:** His appearance and calm manner
- **Supporting point 2:** His clear and patient way of teaching
- **Supporting point 3:** His care for every student
- **Concluding sentence:** In conclusion, Mr. Rahman's kindness and patience made him a teacher I will never forget.
--- answer Paragraph
My mathematics teacher in secondary school, Mr. Rahman, was the kindest and most inspiring teacher I have known. First of all, his appearance and manner made students feel calm. He was a tall man with grey hair and thick glasses, and he always wore a neatly ironed white shirt. He spoke in a soft, steady voice and almost never raised it, even when the class became noisy. Moreover, he taught difficult topics with remarkable clarity and patience. For example, when we struggled with algebra, he drew simple diagrams on the blackboard and explained each step slowly until even the weakest student understood. He used to say, "A question is never foolish." Finally, he cared about every student as a person. If someone looked worried, he would quietly ask about their problems after class, and he often stayed late to help students prepare for board examinations without asking for any payment. In conclusion, Mr. Rahman's kindness and patience made him a teacher I will never forget.
--- note
Describing a person in the **past** uses the past tense throughout. The three points move from **outside to inside**: appearance, then teaching, then character. One short quotation brings the person to life.
:::

:::model Model paragraph 4 · A season | A Winter Morning in the Village
--- outline
- **Topic:** A winter morning in the village
- **Topic sentence:** A winter morning in a Bangladeshi village is calm, misty and full of simple pleasures.
- **Supporting point 1:** The fog and cold air
- **Supporting point 2:** The quiet sounds of the morning
- **Supporting point 3:** Winter food and warmth
- **Concluding sentence:** To sum up, a winter morning in the village offers a peaceful beauty that city life rarely provides.
--- answer Paragraph
A winter morning in a Bangladeshi village is calm, misty and full of simple pleasures. First of all, a thick white fog covers everything at dawn. The paddy fields, the narrow mud roads and the distant trees disappear behind a soft grey curtain, and drops of dew shine like pearls on the grass. The cold air bites the cheeks, so villagers wrap themselves in shawls and blankets. Moreover, the morning is wonderfully quiet. One can hear only the crowing of roosters, the creak of a tube well and the soft murmur of farmers talking as they walk to the fields. Finally, winter mornings bring special food and warmth. Families sit around small straw fires in the courtyard, rubbing their hands over the flames while the smell of smoke drifts through the cold air. Mothers make steaming bhapa pitha, and children wait eagerly for fresh date juice collected from the trees before sunrise. Its sweet, cool taste is the true flavour of winter. To sum up, a winter morning in the village offers a peaceful beauty that city life rarely provides.
--- note
This model uses **all five senses**: sight (fog, dew), touch (cold air, fire), sound (roosters, tube well), smell (straw smoke) and taste (date juice). Figurative language (*dew shine like pearls*, *a soft grey curtain*) is used sparingly, which is the right amount for an exam paragraph.
:::

## Exam strategy

- **Interpret the prompt.** A descriptive topic usually names a person, place, object or time. If it says *"a place where…"*, describe a real place, not an event that happened there.
- **Organise fast.** Use a sense table: write the five senses in a column and fill two words beside each in one minute. Pick the three richest rows.
- **Choose relevant details.** Every detail must support the dominant impression. A detail that fights it ("it is peaceful, but the traffic is terrible") weakens the paragraph unless you deliberately contrast it.
- **Avoid common errors:** mixing tenses (describing a place in the present, then switching to the past), lists of adjectives with no examples, and telling instead of showing.
- **Time:** 3 minutes outline, 12 to 15 minutes writing, 2 minutes checking.
- **Unfamiliar topic?** Every descriptive topic fits the same frame: *impression → feature 1 → feature 2 → feature 3 → restated impression*. Even "a bus stop" can be *crowded and impatient*.

:::practice Practice 3 · Exam level | Outline and paragraph
Write a descriptive paragraph of 150 to 200 words on **"A crowded bazaar on a weekend"**. Show your outline in the exam format.
--- skill
The full midterm task for a descriptive topic
--- hint
Dominant impression: *busy and colourful*. Possible points: the stalls and their goods (sight), the bargaining and noise (sound), the smells of food and spices (smell).
--- answer
**Topic:** A crowded bazaar on a weekend
**Topic sentence:** On weekends, our local bazaar becomes one of the busiest and most colourful places in the town.
**Supporting point 1:** Stalls full of colourful goods
**Supporting point 2:** The noise of bargaining
**Supporting point 3:** The smells of food and spices
**Concluding sentence:** In conclusion, the weekend bazaar is a lively celebration of everyday life.

On weekends, our local bazaar becomes one of the busiest and most colourful places in the town. First of all, the stalls overflow with colourful goods. Mountains of red tomatoes, green chillies and purple aubergines sit beside baskets of silver fish, while cloth sellers hang bright saris and scarves above their shops. Moreover, the bazaar is full of noise. Sellers call out their prices, customers bargain loudly, and rickshaw bells ring as drivers try to pass through the narrow lanes. For example, it is common to hear a buyer and a fish seller argue cheerfully over ten taka for several minutes. Finally, the air is filled with strong smells. The sweet smell of ripe bananas mixes with the sharp smell of dried fish and the warm scent of cumin and coriander from the spice stalls. Near the entrance, fresh singaras sizzle in hot oil and tempt every passer-by. In conclusion, the weekend bazaar is a lively celebration of everyday life.
