# Classification Paragraph

## What it is

The slides define it: *a classification paragraph is a paragraph in which you divide people, things, ideas, or situations into different groups or categories based on shared characteristics.*

The exam sometimes calls it **classification or division**. The two words describe the same task from two directions:

- **Classification** sorts many items into groups (*students → hardworking, social, last-minute*).
- **Division** breaks one thing into its parts (*a university → academic, administrative and student life*).

## The structure from the slides

| Part | What to write |
|---|---|
| Topic sentence | Introduces the subject **and names the categories** |
| Category 1 | Explains the first group with examples and details |
| Category 2 | Explains the second group with examples and details |
| Category 3 | Explains the third group with examples and details |
| Concluding sentence | Summarises the classification |

### The most important rule: one basis

Every classification needs **one principle of division**, a single question that sorts all the items.

| Subject | Basis (question) | Categories |
|---|---|---|
| Students | How do they study? | hardworking, social, last-minute |
| Transport | Where does it travel? | road, rail, water |
| Restaurants | What do they cost? | budget, mid-range, luxury |

**Mixing bases is the classic error.** "Students can be hardworking, social or *from Chattogram*" mixes study habits with home town. The categories must not overlap.

### The slide example, analysed

The slides classify **Types of Students**:

| Part | Text |
|---|---|
| Topic sentence | Students in a university can generally be classified into three types: the hardworking student, the social student, and the last-minute student. |
| Category 1 | First, the hardworking student attends classes regularly, takes careful notes… For example, this type of student usually reviews the lessons after class… As a result, hardworking students are often well-prepared… |
| Category 2 | Second, the social student enjoys spending time with friends… For instance, this student may join clubs… |
| Category 3 | Finally, the last-minute student usually delays studying… For example, such a student may start preparing for an examination only one or two nights before it… Consequently, this type of student often experiences stress… |
| Concluding sentence | Therefore, although students have different habits and personalities, these three categories are commonly found in university classrooms. |

Each category follows the same pattern: **definition → example → result**. That parallel pattern makes the paragraph easy to follow.

## Step-by-step method

1. **Choose the basis** (one question) that sorts the subject clearly.
2. **Name three categories** that do not overlap and cover the subject reasonably well.
3. **Write a topic sentence that names all three** categories, in the order you will discuss them.
4. **Develop each category in the same way:** what it is → a typical example → a result or feature.
5. **Signal each category:** *First, The first type…; Second, Another type…; Finally, The last type…*
6. **Conclude** by summarising the categories or commenting on them.

## Revision summary

> **Topic sentence** (subject + basis + three categories) → **Category 1**: definition, example, result → **Category 2**: same pattern → **Category 3**: same pattern → **concluding sentence**.
>
> One basis, no overlap, parallel development, signal words *First / Second / Finally / Therefore*.

## Model paragraphs

Four models with four different bases: behaviour, travel medium, loyalty and purpose.

:::model Model paragraph 1 · Basis: behaviour | Types of Social Media Users
--- outline
- **Topic:** Types of social media users
- **Topic sentence:** Based on how they use their accounts, social media users can be divided into three types: the creators, the active participants and the silent observers.
- **Supporting point 1:** Creators
- **Supporting point 2:** Active participants
- **Supporting point 3:** Silent observers
- **Concluding sentence:** Therefore, although everyone uses the same platforms, people play very different roles on them.
--- answer Paragraph
Based on how they use their accounts, social media users can be divided into three types: the creators, the active participants and the silent observers. First, creators produce original content for others to enjoy. They regularly post videos, photographs, articles or tutorials and work hard to build a large following. For example, a student who uploads weekly cooking videos or programming tutorials is a creator. As a result, creators often gain influence and sometimes even earn money from their work. Second, active participants spend most of their time reacting to other people's posts. They like, comment, share and join discussions, but they rarely create anything new themselves. For instance, such users may write dozens of comments on a cricket page during a match. Finally, silent observers simply scroll through their feeds without leaving any trace. They read news, watch videos and follow their friends' lives, but they seldom post or comment. Consequently, their friends may not even know how often they are online. Therefore, although everyone uses the same platforms, people play very different roles on them.
--- note
The **basis** (*how they use their accounts*) is stated in the topic sentence, and the three categories do not overlap. Each category follows the slides' pattern: **definition → example (For example/For instance) → result (As a result/Consequently)**.
:::

:::model Model paragraph 2 · Basis: travel medium | Kinds of Public Transport in Bangladesh
--- outline
- **Topic:** Kinds of public transport in Bangladesh
- **Topic sentence:** Public transport in Bangladesh can be classified into three main kinds according to where it travels: road, rail and water transport.
- **Supporting point 1:** Road transport
- **Supporting point 2:** Rail transport
- **Supporting point 3:** Water transport
- **Concluding sentence:** In conclusion, each kind of transport has its own advantages, and together they connect the whole country.
--- answer Paragraph
Public transport in Bangladesh can be classified into three main kinds according to where it travels: road, rail and water transport. First, road transport is the most common kind. It includes buses, rickshaws, CNG auto-rickshaws and, in Dhaka, ride-sharing motorbikes. Road transport reaches almost every corner of the country, but it often suffers from traffic jams. For example, a short bus journey across Dhaka can take more than an hour during rush hour. Second, rail transport offers a safer and more comfortable way to travel long distances. Intercity trains connect major cities such as Dhaka, Chattogram, Sylhet and Rajshahi, and the new metro rail has reduced travel time within the capital. Moreover, trains are usually cheaper than buses for long journeys. Finally, water transport is essential in a riverine country like Bangladesh. Launches, steamers and small boats carry passengers and goods, especially to the southern districts. For instance, many people travel from Dhaka to Barishal by launch overnight. In conclusion, each kind of transport has its own advantages, and together they connect the whole country.
--- note
A **division** of one subject (public transport) by one clear basis (*where it travels*). Specific Bangladeshi examples make each category concrete. The concluding sentence comments on the categories instead of repeating them word for word.
:::

:::model Model paragraph 3 · Basis: loyalty and depth | Types of Friends
--- outline
- **Topic:** Types of friends
- **Topic sentence:** Depending on how close and loyal they are, friends can be classified into three types: acquaintances, casual friends and true friends.
- **Supporting point 1:** Acquaintances
- **Supporting point 2:** Casual friends
- **Supporting point 3:** True friends
- **Concluding sentence:** To sum up, while all three types of friends make life pleasant, true friends are the rarest and most valuable.
--- answer Paragraph
Depending on how close and loyal they are, friends can be classified into three types: acquaintances, casual friends and true friends. The first type, acquaintances, are people we know only slightly. We greet them, exchange a few words and perhaps follow them on social media, but we know little about their lives. For example, a student from another department whom we meet only in the library is usually an acquaintance. The second type is casual friends. We enjoy spending time with them, studying together, watching a match or going out for tea, but we rarely share our deepest problems with them. As a result, these friendships are pleasant but often fade when circumstances change, such as after graduation. The third type, true friends, are the people who stay with us in both good and difficult times. They listen without judging, tell us the truth even when it is hard to hear and help without expecting anything in return. For instance, a true friend will sit with you in the hospital at midnight. To sum up, while all three types of friends make life pleasant, true friends are the rarest and most valuable.
--- note
The categories are arranged in **order of importance**, from the least close to the closest, so the paragraph builds towards its strongest point. Variety in signal phrases (*The first type…, The second type…, The third type…*) avoids repeating *First, Second, Finally* in every model.
:::

:::model Model paragraph 4 · Basis: purpose | Types of Physical Exercise
--- outline
- **Topic:** Types of physical exercise
- **Topic sentence:** According to their main purpose, physical exercises can be divided into three types: aerobic exercise, strength training and flexibility exercise.
- **Supporting point 1:** Aerobic exercise
- **Supporting point 2:** Strength training
- **Supporting point 3:** Flexibility exercise
- **Concluding sentence:** Therefore, a balanced fitness routine should include all three types of exercise.
--- answer Paragraph
According to their main purpose, physical exercises can be divided into three types: aerobic exercise, strength training and flexibility exercise. First, aerobic exercise strengthens the heart and lungs. It includes activities that raise the heart rate for a long period, such as brisk walking, cycling, swimming and skipping. For example, thirty minutes of fast walking five days a week can improve stamina and help control weight. Second, strength training builds and maintains muscle. It involves working the muscles against resistance, using weights, resistance bands or simply one's own body weight in exercises like push-ups and squats. As a result, muscles and bones become stronger, and everyday tasks such as carrying heavy bags become easier. Finally, flexibility exercise keeps the joints and muscles loose. Stretching and yoga are common examples, and they are especially helpful for students who sit at a desk for many hours. Consequently, flexibility exercises reduce stiffness and lower the risk of injury. Therefore, a balanced fitness routine should include all three types of exercise.
--- note
This model classifies by **purpose**, a useful basis for academic subjects. Each category has a **definition, examples and a benefit**, and the concluding sentence draws a practical conclusion from the classification.
:::

## Exam strategy

- **Interpret the prompt.** Classification topics often use *"types of…"*, *"kinds of…"*, *"methods of…"* or *"categories of…"*. If the topic does not suggest categories, create them with one clear basis.
- **Organise fast.** Write the subject, then the question that sorts it, then three answers. Check that no item could belong to two categories.
- **Choose relevant details.** Give every category the same kind of development (definition, example, result). An unbalanced paragraph with one long category and two short ones loses marks.
- **Avoid common errors:** mixing bases, overlapping categories, not naming the categories in the topic sentence, and describing only one category in detail.
- **Time:** 3 minutes outline, 12 to 15 minutes writing, 2 minutes checking.
- **Unfamiliar topic?** Almost anything can be classified by **cost, purpose, behaviour, size, frequency or method**. Choose the basis that gives you the clearest examples.

## Practice

:::practice Practice 1 · Error identification | Find the overlapping category
Which category breaks the classification? Explain why.

**Types of mobile phone users:** a) users who make calls only, b) users who use social media heavily, c) users who own a Samsung phone, d) users who use the phone mainly for work.
--- skill
Keeping one basis of classification
--- answer
**c) users who own a Samsung phone.** The other categories are based on *how people use* their phones; this one is based on *brand*, a different basis. A Samsung owner could also belong to any of the other groups, so the categories overlap.
:::

:::practice Practice 3 · Exam level | Outline and paragraph
Write a classification paragraph of 150 to 200 words on **"Types of restaurants in a city"**. Show your outline in the exam format.
--- skill
The full midterm task for a classification topic
--- hint
Basis: cost or service style. Categories could be street food stalls, casual family restaurants and fine-dining restaurants. Develop each with an example and a result.
--- answer
**Topic:** Types of restaurants in a city
**Topic sentence:** Based on their price and style of service, restaurants in a city can be classified into three types: street food stalls, casual family restaurants and fine-dining restaurants.
**Supporting point 1:** Street food stalls
**Supporting point 2:** Casual family restaurants
**Supporting point 3:** Fine-dining restaurants
**Concluding sentence:** In conclusion, these three types of restaurants allow people of every budget and taste to enjoy eating out.

Based on their price and style of service, restaurants in a city can be classified into three types: street food stalls, casual family restaurants and fine-dining restaurants. First, street food stalls serve quick and cheap snacks. Fuchka, chotpoti and jhalmuri sellers stand on footpaths and near schools, and customers usually eat standing up. As a result, students and office workers can enjoy a tasty snack for less than fifty taka. Second, casual family restaurants offer full meals at a moderate price. They have simple furniture and waiters, and their menus include rice, curries, biryani and fast food. For example, many families visit such restaurants on weekends to celebrate birthdays without spending too much. Finally, fine-dining restaurants provide expensive food in an elegant setting. They are usually located in hotels or wealthy areas and offer special cuisines, careful presentation and formal service. Consequently, people often choose them for business meetings or important occasions. In conclusion, these three types of restaurants allow people of every budget and taste to enjoy eating out.

*(176 words.)*
:::
