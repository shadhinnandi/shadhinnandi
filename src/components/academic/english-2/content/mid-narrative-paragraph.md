# Narrative Paragraph

## What it is

The slides define it: *a narrative paragraph tells a story or describes a sequence of events, usually based on a real or imagined experience.*

Features from the slides:

1. Tells a story.
2. Has a clear **beginning, middle and ending**.
3. Follows **chronological order** (the order in which events happened).
4. Includes **characters, setting and events**.
5. May include **dialogue and emotions**.
6. Usually written in the **past tense**.

A narrative paragraph is not just a list of events. It has a **point**: the experience was exciting, frightening, funny or important. The topic sentence states that point, and the concluding sentence reflects on it.

## The structure

| Part | What to write |
|---|---|
| Topic sentence | The experience + how it felt or why it mattered (*was both exciting and challenging*) |
| Supporting point 1 | **Beginning:** the setting and the first event |
| Supporting point 2 | **Middle:** the main event, problem or turning point |
| Supporting point 3 | **Ending:** how it was resolved |
| Concluding sentence | Reflection: what it meant, what you learned or felt |

### The slide example, analysed

The slides narrate **My First Day at University**:

| Part | Text |
|---|---|
| Topic sentence | My first day at university was both exciting and challenging. |
| Supporting detail 1 (beginning) | Firstly, I arrived on campus early in the morning feeling nervous because everything was new to me. However, the beautiful campus and friendly atmosphere gradually made me feel more comfortable. |
| Supporting detail 2 (middle) | Next, I met several new classmates, and we introduced ourselves… The teacher warmly welcomed us, explained the course outline… |
| Supporting detail 3 (end) | Finally, during the break, I explored different parts of the campus with my new friends… By the time I returned home, I felt much more confident… |
| Concluding sentence | In conclusion, my first day at university was a memorable experience that I will always cherish. |

The story moves in time order (*Firstly → Next → Finally*), includes feelings (*nervous*, *comfortable*, *confident*) and ends with a reflection.

## Step-by-step method

1. **Choose one short experience** that happened in a few hours or a day. A whole year is too long for one paragraph.
2. **Decide the point:** what feeling or lesson ties the story together? Put it in the topic sentence.
3. **Divide the story into three stages:** beginning, middle (the turning point), ending.
4. **Add the five Ws** to each stage: who, what, when, where, why.
5. **Include feelings and one line of dialogue** if it helps; keep it short.
6. **Use time transitions:** *First, At first, Then, Next, Suddenly, After that, Later, Finally, By the time…*
7. **Keep the past tense** throughout. Switching to the present by mistake is the most common narrative error (and it is an editing category: *inconsistent verb tense*).

## Revision summary

> **Topic sentence** (experience + feeling/point) → **beginning** → **middle / turning point** → **ending** → **concluding reflection**.
>
> Chronological order, time transitions, past tense, characters + setting + events, emotions, optional dialogue.

## Model paragraphs

Four models: a challenge, a mistake, a kind act and a small victory. Each one is a single short experience with a clear point.

:::model Model paragraph 1 · A challenge | My First Public Speech
--- outline
- **Topic:** My first public speech
- **Topic sentence:** Giving my first speech in front of the whole school was one of the most frightening yet rewarding experiences of my life.
- **Supporting point 1:** Nervous preparation and waiting backstage
- **Supporting point 2:** A mistake at the start and how I recovered
- **Supporting point 3:** The applause and the feeling afterwards
- **Concluding sentence:** In the end, that speech taught me that courage means acting even when you are afraid.
--- answer Paragraph
Giving my first speech in front of the whole school was one of the most frightening yet rewarding experiences of my life. At first, I spent a whole week practising my speech on Victory Day in front of a mirror, but on the morning of the programme my hands were shaking. While I waited behind the stage, I could hear hundreds of students talking, and my heart began to race. Then my name was announced, and I walked to the microphone. As soon as I looked at the audience, I forgot my first line completely. For a few seconds, there was total silence. Suddenly, I noticed my class teacher in the front row, who smiled and nodded at me. I took a deep breath, looked at my notes and started again slowly. Finally, when I finished, the hall filled with applause, and my friends cheered loudly. I walked off the stage feeling proud and light, as if a heavy weight had been lifted from my shoulders. In the end, that speech taught me that courage means acting even when you are afraid.
--- note
The **turning point** (forgetting the first line, then seeing the teacher) is the middle of the story and creates suspense. Feelings appear at every stage: *shaking*, *race*, *proud and light*. Notice the consistent **past tense** and the time words *At first, Then, Suddenly, Finally, In the end*.
:::

:::model Model paragraph 2 · A mistake | The Day I Missed the Last Bus
--- outline
- **Topic:** The day I missed the last bus
- **Topic sentence:** The evening I missed the last bus home taught me a valuable lesson about planning.
- **Supporting point 1:** Staying too late at a friend's house
- **Supporting point 2:** Reaching the bus stop too late and feeling stranded
- **Supporting point 3:** Calling my father and the long journey home
- **Concluding sentence:** Since that night, I have always checked the time and planned my journey before going out.
--- answer Paragraph
The evening I missed the last bus home taught me a valuable lesson about planning. First, I went to my friend's house in Gazipur to work on a group assignment. We became so busy discussing our project and watching videos that none of us noticed the time. When I finally looked at my phone, it was already a quarter to ten. Next, I ran to the bus stop as fast as I could, but the road was empty. A shopkeeper who was closing his shutter told me, "The last bus left ten minutes ago." I stood there in the dark, feeling worried and foolish, with very little money in my pocket. After that, I called my father, who was angry at first but agreed to come and pick me up. While I waited for more than an hour on a bench beside the closed shop, I promised myself that I would never be so careless again. Since that night, I have always checked the time and planned my journey before going out.
--- note
A narrative can be about a **mistake**. The point (*a lesson about planning*) is stated in the topic sentence and returned to in the concluding sentence. One line of **dialogue** with correct quotation marks makes the moment vivid.
:::

:::model Model paragraph 3 · A kind act | Helping a Stranger on a Flooded Road
--- outline
- **Topic:** Helping a stranger on a flooded road
- **Topic sentence:** One rainy afternoon, a small act of help on a flooded road made my whole day meaningful.
- **Supporting point 1:** The heavy rain and the flooded road
- **Supporting point 2:** Seeing an old man in trouble and helping him
- **Supporting point 3:** His gratitude and my feelings on the way home
- **Concluding sentence:** That afternoon showed me that even a small kindness can make a big difference to someone.
--- answer Paragraph
One rainy afternoon, a small act of help on a flooded road made my whole day meaningful. It was July, and a heavy monsoon shower had turned the road near our university into a shallow river. Rickshaws moved slowly through the knee-deep water, and people stood under shop roofs waiting for the rain to stop. Then I noticed an elderly man trying to cross the road with a bag of groceries. Halfway across, his foot slipped, and some of his vegetables floated away. Without thinking, I stepped into the water, held his arm and helped him reach the other side. Two other students quickly collected his vegetables and returned them. Finally, the old man thanked us again and again and placed his hand on my head to bless me. Although my shoes and trousers were soaking wet, I walked home smiling and felt happier than I had in weeks. That afternoon showed me that even a small kindness can make a big difference to someone.
--- note
The **setting** (July, monsoon, flooded road) is established quickly in the beginning. The middle has a clear **problem** (the man slips), and the ending shows the **result** and the writer's emotion. The paragraph shows character through action rather than saying "I am a kind person".
:::

:::model Model paragraph 4 · A small victory | Learning to Swim at Twelve
--- outline
- **Topic:** Learning to swim at twelve
- **Topic sentence:** Learning to swim in our village pond was a slow and difficult journey, but it gave me great confidence.
- **Supporting point 1:** My fear of water at the beginning
- **Supporting point 2:** My uncle's patient lessons and my progress
- **Supporting point 3:** The day I swam across the pond alone
- **Concluding sentence:** To conclude, that summer taught me that patience and practice can overcome any fear.
--- answer Paragraph
Learning to swim in our village pond was a slow and difficult journey, but it gave me great confidence. At the beginning of the summer holidays, I was afraid even to put my feet in the dark water. My cousins swam and laughed around me, while I sat on the steps of the ghat and pretended I did not want to join them. Then my uncle decided to teach me. Every morning for two weeks, he held me under my stomach and showed me how to kick my legs and move my arms. At first, I swallowed a lot of water and wanted to give up, but slowly I learned to float and breathe calmly. Finally, on the last day of the holidays, I swam from one side of the pond to the other without any help. When I reached the far bank, my cousins clapped and shouted my name, and I felt like a champion. To conclude, that summer taught me that patience and practice can overcome any fear.
--- note
A story that develops over **two weeks** still fits one paragraph because it is focused on one goal. The three stages show **change**: fear → practice → success. Time expressions (*At the beginning, Every morning for two weeks, At first, Finally*) keep the order clear.
:::

## Exam strategy

- **Interpret the prompt.** Narrative topics often begin with *"An experience…"*, *"A day when…"*, *"A memorable…"*, *"The first time…"*. They ask for **your** story, so use "I" and the past tense.
- **Organise fast.** Draw three boxes: *Beginning / Turning point / Ending*. Write three words in each. That is your outline.
- **Choose relevant details.** Include only events that lead to the turning point. Skip what you had for breakfast unless it matters.
- **Avoid common errors:** tense switching (*"I went… and I see…"*), a story with no point, a story too long for one paragraph, and dialogue without quotation marks.
- **Time:** 3 minutes outline, 12 to 15 minutes writing, 2 minutes checking verbs.
- **Unfamiliar topic?** Any narrative fits *feeling/point → beginning → turning point → ending → reflection*. If you cannot remember a real event, a realistic imagined one is acceptable.

## Practice

:::practice Practice 1 · Error identification | Fix the tense
Correct the verbs so the whole passage stays in the past tense.

*Last Friday, I went to the stadium with my brother. When we arrive, the gates are already closed, so we wait outside for an hour.*
--- skill
Keeping consistent past tense in a narrative
--- answer
*Last Friday, I went to the stadium with my brother. When we **arrived**, the gates **were** already closed, so we **waited** outside for an hour.*
:::

:::practice Practice 2 · Guided | Three-stage plan
Plan the story **"The day I lost my phone"** in three stages, with one feeling at each stage.
--- skill
Organising a narrative into beginning, middle and ending
--- answer
- **Beginning:** On a crowded bus home from class, I put my phone in my bag. *(Feeling: relaxed, tired.)*
- **Middle / turning point:** At home, I could not find it; I called it from my sister's phone, and a stranger answered. *(Feeling: panic, then hope.)*
- **Ending:** The stranger, a bus conductor, kept it safe, and I collected it the next morning. *(Feeling: relief and gratitude.)*
- **Point:** Honest people still exist, and I learned to be more careful.
:::

Write a narrative paragraph of 150 to 200 words on **"A journey I will never forget"**. Show your outline in the exam format.
--- skill
The full midterm task for a narrative topic
--- hint
Pick one journey (a launch trip, a train journey, a long bus ride). Plan: start of journey → something unexpected → arrival. State in the topic sentence why it was unforgettable.
--- answer
**Topic:** A journey I will never forget
**Topic sentence:** My first launch journey from Dhaka to Barishal was an unforgettable adventure.
**Supporting point 1:** Boarding the crowded launch at Sadarghat
**Supporting point 2:** A sudden storm on the river at night
**Supporting point 3:** A calm sunrise and our arrival
**Concluding sentence:** Even today, that journey reminds me of both the power and the beauty of our rivers.

My first launch journey from Dhaka to Barishal was an unforgettable adventure. In the evening, my family and I boarded a huge three-storey launch at Sadarghat. The terminal was crowded with porters, passengers and hawkers, and the launch blew its deep horn as it slowly left the shore. At first, I stood on the deck and watched the city lights fade away. Around midnight, however, a strong storm suddenly hit the river. The wind howled, rain lashed the windows and the launch began to rock from side to side. Many passengers prayed loudly, and my little sister held my hand tightly. After nearly an hour, the storm passed, and the river became calm again. Finally, I woke up early and saw the sun rising over the wide, silver river. Fishermen were already casting their nets, and soon we reached Barishal safely. Even today, that journey reminds me of both the power and the beauty of our rivers.
