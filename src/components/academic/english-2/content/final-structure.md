## The topics in detail

### Reading tasks

The final keeps multiple choice and short answers from the midterm and adds two new tasks. **True/False/Not Given** asks whether a statement agrees with the passage, contradicts it or is not mentioned. **Making sentences** asks you to use given words correctly and meaningfully. **Why it matters:** both tasks reward careful reading and precise vocabulary, and both are easy to lose marks on through guessing.

### Essay structure and outline

The slides teach one outline used for every essay type: topic, thesis statement, supporting points (plan of development), the topic sentence of each body paragraph and the concluding sentence. You will learn what each part of the introduction does (hook, general statement, background, thesis) and how body paragraphs and the conclusion relate to the thesis. **Why it matters:** the outline is marked separately and the essay must follow it.

### Thesis statement writing

A thesis states the main point of the essay and lists its three supporting points in parallel form. **Why it matters:** a strong thesis organises the whole essay, and question 2B asks you to complete thesis statements with a matching third point.

### Thesis statement editing

You will learn to recognise four kinds of statement: an **announcement** (A), a statement **too narrow** to develop (TN), one **too broad** to develop (TB) and a clear thesis (OK). **Why it matters:** question 2A tests exactly this, and the same skill helps you check your own thesis.

### The four essay types

- **Descriptive:** describes a subject through three aspects, with sensory detail (slides: *A Rainy Day*).
- **Cause and effect:** explains causes, effects or both (slides: *Natural Disasters*, in three versions).
- **Argumentative:** takes a clear position, supports it with three reasons and refutes the opposing view (slides: *Should Attendance Be Mandatory?*, for and against).
- **Compare and contrast:** examines similarities, differences or both (slides: *Online and Offline Classes*, in three versions).

**Why it matters:** the essay is 10 marks, and the exam labels each topic with its type, so you must know each structure.

### Editing: eight errors

The same categories as the midterm, plus **faulty parallelism**, with eight numbered errors. **Why it matters:** 4 marks for careful, methodical checking.

### Applications and emails

You will learn the exact format of the sample application (date, recipient, subject, salutation, body, closing, signature block) and the sample email (To, Subject, salutation, body, closing). **Why it matters:** 3 marks, and the format alone earns a large share.

### Vocabulary

150 words in three levels with Bengali meanings and example sentences, in groups of ten. **Why it matters:** making correct sentences with unfamiliar words is a direct reading-part task, and a wider vocabulary improves every essay.
