# Final Introduction

The final builds directly on the midterm. A paragraph becomes the body paragraph of an essay, the topic sentence grows into the thesis statement, and the editing skills stay the same with more errors to find. The final also adds formal writing (an application or an email) and a sentence-making task that tests vocabulary.

## What the final covers

| Part | Marks | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Reading** | 9 | Multiple choice, true/false/not given, making sentences with given words, short answers in your own words |
| **B. Writing** | 21 | Classify thesis statements (2), complete thesis statements (2), find and correct eight errors (4), write an application or an email (3), outline and write an essay (2 + 8) |
| Listening and speaking | 10 | Tested separately in class |

The essay alone is worth 10 marks, a quarter of the paper. Thesis statements, the outline and the essay together make up 14 of the 21 writing marks.

## The skills behind the marks

- **Essay structure.** An essay has an introduction ending in a thesis statement, three body paragraphs (each one a full midterm paragraph) and a conclusion. The final slides teach a fixed outline that you must write before the essay.
- **Thesis statements.** The thesis states the essay's main point and its plan of development. You must recognise weak thesis statements (announcements, too broad, too narrow) and complete a thesis with a parallel third point.
- **Essay types.** The slides model four types: descriptive, cause and effect, argumentative and compare and contrast. The exam offers a choice of three topics, each labelled with its type.
- **Formal writing.** Applications and emails each follow the format of the sample in the final materials.
- **Editing.** Eight numbered errors this time, with faulty parallelism added to the familiar categories.
- **Vocabulary.** The reading part asks you to make grammatically correct and meaningful sentences with words such as those in the vocabulary lesson.

## How the topics connect

| Midterm skill | Becomes in the final |
|---|---|
| Topic sentence + controlling idea | Thesis statement + plan of development |
| Three supporting points | Three body paragraphs |
| Concluding sentence | Concluding paragraph |
| Descriptive / cause-effect paragraph | Descriptive / cause-effect essay |
| Four editing errors | Eight editing errors |

If the midterm paragraph lessons feel shaky, revise **Paragraph Structure** before starting the essay lessons.

## How to use the final lessons

1. Read **Final Course Structure** for the full map.
2. Study **Essay Structure and Outline** and the two **thesis** lessons before any essay type: every essay depends on them.
3. Work through the four essay types, then Editing, Applications, Emails and Vocabulary.
4. Do every practice task on paper. For essays, time yourself: outline in 5 minutes, essay in 25 to 30.
5. Finish with the real Spring 2026 final paper under exam time, then compare with the full solutions.

> **Time plan for the 2-hour exam (about 100 minutes for reading and writing):** reading 20 minutes · thesis tasks 8 minutes · editing 8 minutes · application or email 12 minutes · essay outline 5 minutes and essay 35 minutes · final check 5 minutes.
