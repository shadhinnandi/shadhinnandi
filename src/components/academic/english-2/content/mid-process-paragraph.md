# Process Paragraph

## What it is

The slides define it: *a process paragraph explains how to do something or how something happens, presenting the steps in a logical sequence.*

Features from the slides:

1. Explains a process **step by step**.
2. Follows **chronological order**.
3. Uses **sequencing words**.
4. Often includes **imperative verbs** (instructions) or **passive voice**.
5. Ensures **each step is clear and complete**.
6. Usually written in the **present tense**.

There are two kinds of process paragraph:

| Kind | Example topic | Typical style |
|---|---|---|
| **Instructional** ("how to do it") | How to prepare for an exam | Imperatives (*Make a timetable…*) or "you" |
| **Informational** ("how it happens / how it is done") | How a paragraph is written; how rice is grown | Passive voice (*the seeds are planted…*) or third person |

The slide model, *The Phases of Writing a Paragraph*, uses the **passive voice**: *"a suitable topic should be selected… the first draft should be written… the paragraph should be revised."*

## The structure

A process may have ten small steps, but your outline has room for three supporting points. The solution is to **group the steps into three stages**.

| Part | What to write |
|---|---|
| Topic sentence | The process + its purpose or result (*involves several important phases that help organise ideas effectively*) |
| Supporting point 1 | **Stage 1:** preparation (materials, planning) |
| Supporting point 2 | **Stage 2:** the main action |
| Supporting point 3 | **Stage 3:** finishing and checking |
| Concluding sentence | The result of following the process |

### The slide example, analysed

| Part | Text |
|---|---|
| Topic sentence | Writing a good paragraph involves several important phases that help organize ideas effectively. |
| Stage 1 | First, a suitable topic should be selected, and the main idea should be planned before writing begins… |
| Stage 2 | Next, the first draft should be written by developing the topic sentence, supporting details, and concluding sentence in a logical order… |
| Stage 3 | Finally, the paragraph should be revised and edited carefully… |
| Concluding sentence | To conclude, by following these phases, a clear, well-organized, and effective paragraph can be produced. |

## Sequencing words

| Stage | Words |
|---|---|
| Beginning | First, First of all, To begin with, Before you start |
| Middle | Next, Then, After that, Second, Once this is done, While…, Meanwhile |
| End | Finally, Lastly, At the end, Once everything is ready |
| Warning / tip | Be careful to…, Make sure that…, It is important to… |

## Step-by-step method

1. **List every step** of the process quickly, in order (aim for 6 to 9).
2. **Group them into three stages**, such as *before, during, after* or *prepare, do, finish*.
3. **Choose one style** and keep it: imperative (*Choose a topic*), "you" (*you choose a topic*) or passive (*a topic is chosen*). Mixing styles is a common error.
4. **For each stage**, explain why it matters or give a tip. This turns a list into a paragraph.
5. **Write the outline**, then the paragraph with sequencing words.
6. **Check completeness:** could a reader follow your paragraph without asking a question?

## Revision summary

> **Topic sentence** (process + purpose) → **Stage 1** prepare → **Stage 2** do → **Stage 3** finish/check → **concluding sentence** (result).
>
> Chronological order, sequencing words, one consistent style (imperative, "you" or passive), present tense, explain *why* each stage matters.

## Model paragraphs

Four models: a study skill, a practical task, an informational (passive voice) process and a digital task.

:::model Model paragraph 1 · Instructional | How to Prepare for an Examination
--- outline
- **Topic:** How to prepare for an examination
- **Topic sentence:** Preparing for an examination becomes much easier when students follow a clear, step-by-step plan.
- **Supporting point 1:** Make a study timetable and collect materials
- **Supporting point 2:** Study actively and practise past-style questions
- **Supporting point 3:** Revise and rest before the exam
- **Concluding sentence:** In conclusion, careful planning, active study and proper rest can turn exam stress into confidence.
--- answer Paragraph
Preparing for an examination becomes much easier when students follow a clear, step-by-step plan. First, make a study timetable at least two weeks before the exam. Divide the syllabus into small topics and give each topic a fixed day, leaving the last two days free for revision. At the same time, collect all the lecture slides, class notes and textbooks, so that no time is wasted searching for materials later. Next, study actively instead of simply reading. For example, after finishing a topic, close the book, write the main points from memory and then check what you missed. It is also important to practise questions in the exam format and to time yourself, because this shows which topics need more work. Finally, revise and rest before the exam. On the last day, read only your short notes and key formulas instead of starting new topics, and sleep for at least seven hours so that your mind is fresh. In conclusion, careful planning, active study and proper rest can turn exam stress into confidence.
--- note
Many small actions are **grouped into three stages** (plan, study, revise). The paragraph uses **imperatives** consistently (*make, divide, collect, study, revise*) and explains *why* each step matters (*so that no time is wasted*, *because this shows…*).
:::

:::model Model paragraph 2 · Instructional | How to Make a Cup of Milk Tea
--- outline
- **Topic:** How to make a cup of milk tea
- **Topic sentence:** Making a perfect cup of milk tea at home is simple if the steps are followed in the right order.
- **Supporting point 1:** Gather the ingredients and boil the water
- **Supporting point 2:** Brew the tea leaves with milk and spices
- **Supporting point 3:** Sweeten, strain and serve
- **Concluding sentence:** By following these simple steps, anyone can enjoy a fresh and fragrant cup of tea in less than ten minutes.
--- answer Paragraph
Making a perfect cup of milk tea at home is simple if the steps are followed in the right order. To begin with, gather all the ingredients: water, loose tea leaves, milk, sugar and, if you like, a piece of ginger or two cardamom pods. Pour one cup of water into a small saucepan and bring it to the boil over medium heat. Next, add one teaspoon of tea leaves and the crushed ginger or cardamom to the boiling water. Let it boil for about two minutes until the water turns a deep reddish brown, and then add half a cup of milk. Be careful to lower the heat at this stage, because milk can boil over very quickly. After that, let the tea simmer for another minute until it becomes creamy. Finally, add sugar according to your taste, stir well and pour the tea through a strainer into a cup. Serve it hot, perhaps with a few biscuits. By following these simple steps, anyone can enjoy a fresh and fragrant cup of tea in less than ten minutes.
--- note
Precise **quantities and times** (*one teaspoon*, *about two minutes*) make each step complete. A **warning** (*Be careful to lower the heat…*) shows the reader why a step matters. Sequencing words lead the reader: *To begin with, Next, After that, Finally*.
:::

:::model Model paragraph 3 · Informational, passive voice | How Rice Is Cultivated in Bangladesh
--- outline
- **Topic:** How rice is cultivated in Bangladesh
- **Topic sentence:** Rice cultivation in Bangladesh is a careful process that is completed in three main stages.
- **Supporting point 1:** Preparing the seedbed and the field
- **Supporting point 2:** Transplanting and caring for the plants
- **Supporting point 3:** Harvesting and processing the crop
- **Concluding sentence:** Thus, through these stages, the rice that feeds the nation is produced by the hard work of farmers.
--- answer Paragraph
Rice cultivation in Bangladesh is a careful process that is completed in three main stages. First, the seedbed and the main field are prepared. Healthy seeds are soaked in water for a day and then sown thickly in a small, well-watered seedbed. Meanwhile, the main field is ploughed several times, flooded with water and levelled so that the soil becomes soft and muddy. Next, the young seedlings are transplanted and carefully looked after. After about a month, they are pulled from the seedbed, tied into bundles and planted in neat rows in the flooded field. During the following months, weeds are removed, fertiliser is applied and the water level is checked regularly. Finally, the crop is harvested and processed. When the grains turn golden, the plants are cut with sickles and carried to the farmer's yard. There, the grain is separated from the stalks, dried in the sun and stored or sent to the mill. Thus, through these stages, the rice that feeds the nation is produced by the hard work of farmers.
--- note
This is an **informational** process, like the slide model, so it uses the **passive voice** (*are soaked*, *is ploughed*, *are transplanted*). The focus is on the process, not on who does it. Each stage contains several small steps, grouped logically.
:::

:::model Model paragraph 4 · Digital task | How to Write a Professional Email to a Teacher
--- outline
- **Topic:** How to write a professional email to a teacher
- **Topic sentence:** Writing a professional email to a teacher is easy when it is done in three clear steps.
- **Supporting point 1:** Plan the purpose and write a clear subject line
- **Supporting point 2:** Write a polite, short body
- **Supporting point 3:** Close properly and check before sending
- **Concluding sentence:** In short, a well-planned, polite and carefully checked email shows respect and gets a faster reply.
--- answer Paragraph
Writing a professional email to a teacher is easy when it is done in three clear steps. First, decide exactly why you are writing and express it in a short, clear subject line. For example, "Request for Extension of Lab Report Submission" is far more useful than "Help" or "Urgent", because the teacher immediately understands the purpose. Next, write a polite and brief body. Begin with a respectful greeting such as "Dear Sir" or "Dear Madam", introduce yourself with your name, course and section, and then explain your purpose in two or three sentences. Avoid slang, emojis and text-message abbreviations such as "plz" or "u". Finally, close the email properly and check it before sending. End with a phrase such as "Sincerely", followed by your full name, student ID and department. Then read the email once more to correct spelling and grammar mistakes, and make sure any necessary file is attached. In short, a well-planned, polite and carefully checked email shows respect and gets a faster reply.
--- note
A process can be about a modern, everyday task. Contrasting good and bad choices (*"Request for Extension…" is far more useful than "Help"*) makes each step clear. This paragraph also previews the **Email** lesson in the final section.
:::

## Exam strategy

- **Interpret the prompt.** Process topics begin with *"How to…"*, *"The steps of…"*, *"Organising…"*, *"Preparing…"*. They want steps in order, not a description or opinion.
- **Organise fast.** Write every step as a word or two in a vertical list, then draw brackets to group them into three stages. Those stages are your three supporting points.
- **Choose relevant details.** Include every step a reader needs and nothing else. Add a short reason or warning for at least two steps.
- **Avoid common errors:** steps out of order, missing steps, mixing imperative and "you" and passive styles, and writing a list with no explanation.
- **Time:** 3 minutes outline, 12 to 15 minutes writing, 2 minutes checking order and verbs.
- **Unfamiliar topic?** Use the universal frame: **prepare → do → finish/check**. It fits cooking, studying, organising, travelling and almost any other process.

## Practice

:::practice Practice 1 · Basic | Put the steps in order
Arrange these steps for **"How to plant a tree"** in the correct order.

a) Water the plant well.
b) Choose a sunny place and dig a hole.
c) Place the sapling in the hole and fill it with soil.
d) Check it regularly and protect it from animals.
e) Mix compost with the soil from the hole.
--- skill
Chronological order
--- answer
**b → e → c → a → d.** Choose and dig, prepare the soil, plant, water, then look after it.
:::

:::practice Practice 2 · Error identification | Fix the mixed style
The writer mixes imperative and passive styles. Rewrite the sentences in the imperative.

*First, choose a quiet room. Then the books should be arranged on the desk. Next, you should switch off your phone.*
--- skill
Keeping one consistent style in a process paragraph
--- answer
*First, choose a quiet room. Then **arrange the books** on the desk. Next, **switch off your phone**.*
:::

:::practice Practice 3 · Exam level | Outline and paragraph
Write a process paragraph of 150 to 200 words on **"How to open a bank account"**. Show your outline in the exam format.
--- skill
The full midterm task for a process topic
--- hint
Group into three stages: collect documents → visit the bank and fill in the form → deposit money and receive the account details.
--- answer
**Topic:** How to open a bank account
**Topic sentence:** Opening a bank account is a simple process if it is completed in three main steps.
**Supporting point 1:** Collect the necessary documents
**Supporting point 2:** Visit the bank and complete the application form
**Supporting point 3:** Make the first deposit and receive the account details
**Concluding sentence:** By following these steps, anyone can open a bank account and begin managing money safely.

Opening a bank account is a simple process if it is completed in three main steps. First, collect all the necessary documents. Most banks require a copy of your national identity card or birth certificate, two passport-size photographs and, for students, a copy of the student ID card. It is wise to call the bank or visit its website first, because requirements can differ. Next, visit the nearest branch and complete the application form. Ask the customer service officer which type of account suits you, such as a student savings account, and then fill in your personal details carefully. Make sure your name matches your identity documents exactly, since even a small spelling difference can cause delays. Finally, make the first deposit and receive your account details. After the officer checks your documents, deposit the minimum amount required, sign the specimen signature card and collect your account number. Within a few days, you will usually receive a cheque book or debit card. By following these steps, anyone can open a bank account and begin managing money safely.

*(184 words.)*
:::
