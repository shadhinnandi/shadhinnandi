## Part A: Reading (12 marks, CO 2)

Read the passage below and answer the following questions.

> In today's fast-paced professional world, the link between how employees feel and how much they achieve is stronger than ever. Motivation is the internal engine that drives people to reach their goals. It is the spark that turns a routine daily task into a meaningful contribution. When a team is truly motivated, they do not just check boxes or watch the clock until the end of the day. Instead they bring creativity, energy, and a sense of purpose to everything they do. This high level of engagement is what powers productivity, leading to better results, fewer mistakes, and a much stronger organization overall.
>
> To understand how to keep people inspired, we have to look at where motivation actually comes from. Generally, it is divided into two main categories known as intrinsic and extrinsic motivation. Intrinsic motivation comes from within a person. It is the natural desire to learn a new skill, the pride of doing a job well, or the genuine joy found in the work itself. On the other hand, extrinsic motivation comes from outside rewards. This includes things like a competitive salary, year-end bonuses, or getting a public promotion. While a fair paycheck is absolutely essential to attract talented people, money alone is rarely enough to keep them performing at their best for many years.
>
> Long-term success is usually fueled by intrinsic factors. Employees are far more likely to stay committed during difficult times when they feel they have autonomy and mastery. Autonomy means having some control over how they do their work, while mastery is the chance to get better at their specific craft. When workers feel a clear connection to the company's bigger mission, they view their labor as a part of a larger success story.
>
> The atmosphere of the workplace plays a massive role in keeping that internal spark alive. A humane workplace is one that treats people like human beings rather than just tools used for profit. This type of environment prioritizes open communication, where people feel comfortable sharing ideas. It also focuses on psychological safety, meaning employees are not afraid to make mistakes or ask for help when they are stuck. Additionally, a healthy work-life balance ensures that the organization respects the fact that people have lives and families outside of their jobs.
>
> When leaders take the time to recognize individual hard work and offer chances for professional growth, they build a culture of deep trust. This sense of belonging is the best defense against burnout and high turnover. When people feel valued and supported, they are much less likely to quit, which allows the company to keep its most experienced and passionate team members.
>
> Ultimately, maximizing productivity is not about using pressure to force people to work harder. In fact, high-pressure environments often lead to stress and lower-quality work in the long run. Real productivity comes from creating the right conditions so that employees naturally want to do their best. By balancing fair pay with a supportive and respectful culture, organizations can unlock the full potential of their people. In a competitive global market, the ability to inspire a team remains the most valuable advantage.

**i. A place where I feel peaceful (Descriptive)**

**Topic:** A place where I feel peaceful
**Topic sentence:** The rooftop of our building at sunset is the place where I feel most peaceful.
**Supporting point 1:** The wide, open view of the sky
**Supporting point 2:** The gentle sounds and cool breeze
**Supporting point 3:** My small corner of plants
**Concluding sentence:** In conclusion, the rooftop at sunset gives me a calm escape from the noise of city life.

The rooftop of our building at sunset is the place where I feel most peaceful. First of all, it offers a wide, open view that I cannot find anywhere else in the crowded city. From the rooftop, the sky turns orange, pink and purple, and flocks of birds fly home across it in long lines. Moreover, the sounds and air there are gentle. The noise of traffic becomes a distant hum, and a cool breeze moves the clothes on the drying lines and touches my face after a hot day. For example, I can often hear the call to prayer and children laughing from a nearby field. Finally, the rooftop has a small corner of potted plants that my mother and I look after. The smell of wet soil and jasmine flowers rises when I water them, and caring for the plants slowly removes my stress. In conclusion, the rooftop at sunset gives me a calm escape from the noise of city life.

**ii. Organizing a successful event (Process)**

**Topic:** Organizing a successful event
**Topic sentence:** Organizing a successful event requires careful work in three main stages.
**Supporting point 1:** Planning the purpose, budget and date
**Supporting point 2:** Dividing tasks and preparing the venue
**Supporting point 3:** Managing the day and reviewing afterwards
**Concluding sentence:** Therefore, careful planning, teamwork and follow-up are the keys to a successful event.

Organizing a successful event requires careful work in three main stages. First, the organizers should plan the purpose, budget and date of the event. They need to decide what the event aims to achieve, how many guests will attend and how much money is available. For example, a department cultural night must be planned at least a month ahead so that the venue can be booked. Next, the tasks should be divided among a team and the venue prepared. One group can handle invitations and publicity, another can arrange food and sound systems, and a third can decorate the hall. Regular short meetings help everyone stay on schedule. Finally, the event must be managed carefully on the day and reviewed afterwards. Volunteers should welcome guests, keep the programme on time and solve problems quickly. After the event, the team should check the accounts and discuss what could be improved next time. Therefore, careful planning, teamwork and follow-up are the keys to a successful event.

**iii. An experience that changed my perspective (Narrative)**

**Topic:** An experience that changed my perspective
**Topic sentence:** A week of volunteering at a flood shelter completely changed my view of what really matters in life.
**Supporting point 1:** Arriving at the crowded shelter with doubts
**Supporting point 2:** Working with families who had lost everything
**Supporting point 3:** Returning home with a new attitude
**Concluding sentence:** Since that week, I have learned to value what I have and to help others whenever I can.

A week of volunteering at a flood shelter completely changed my view of what really matters in life. At first, I joined my university's relief team mainly because my friends were going. When we arrived at the school building used as a shelter in Sylhet, I was shocked to see hundreds of people sleeping on classroom floors. Then, over the next few days, I helped distribute food, clean water and medicine. I met an old farmer who had lost his house and his cows, yet he smiled and thanked every volunteer. A young mother told me, "We have nothing now, but my children are safe, and that is enough." Her words stayed in my mind. Finally, I returned home and looked at my comfortable room differently. I felt ashamed of how often I had complained about small problems, such as slow internet or ordinary meals. Since that week, I have learned to value what I have and to help others whenever I can.

**iv. Daily life communications methods (Classification or Division)**

**Topic:** Daily life communication methods
**Topic sentence:** The methods people use to communicate in daily life can be divided into three types: face-to-face, written and digital communication.
**Supporting point 1:** Face-to-face communication
**Supporting point 2:** Written communication
**Supporting point 3:** Digital communication
**Concluding sentence:** Therefore, each method of communication has its own purpose, and people choose among them every day.

The methods people use to communicate in daily life can be divided into three types: face-to-face, written and digital communication. First, face-to-face communication happens when people speak directly to each other. Family conversations at dinner, discussions in class and bargaining in the market are common examples. Because people can see facial expressions and hear the tone of voice, this method is the most personal and least likely to cause misunderstanding. Second, written communication uses paper documents such as letters, applications, notices and reports. For instance, a student writes an application to the head of the department for leave. As a result, written communication is useful when a permanent, formal record is needed. Finally, digital communication uses technology such as mobile phones, email, messaging apps and video calls. It allows people to contact others instantly across long distances, so families can talk to relatives working abroad every day. Therefore, each method of communication has its own purpose, and people choose among them every day.
