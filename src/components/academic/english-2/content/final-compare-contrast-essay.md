# Compare and Contrast Essay

## Definition and purpose

A compare and contrast essay **examines two subjects side by side** to show how they are alike, how they differ, or both.

- **Comparing** means showing **similarities**.
- **Contrasting** means showing **differences**.

The purpose is not to describe each subject separately but to **set them against each other point by point**, so the reader understands both more clearly. Often the essay ends by saying which subject suits which person or situation.

The slides model one pair, *online and offline classes*, in **three versions**:

| Slide | Focus | Thesis |
|---|---|---|
| 4.1 Similarities | **Comparison only** | *Both online and offline classes provide students with opportunities to gain knowledge, interact with teachers and classmates, and fulfill academic responsibilities.* |
| 4.2 Offline vs. online | **Contrast only** | *Offline and online classes differ significantly in terms of interaction, flexibility, and learning environment.* |
| 4. Compare and contrast | **Both** | *Although online and offline classes share several similarities, they differ significantly in terms of interaction, flexibility, and learning environment.* |

Read the topic carefully. *"Similarities between…"* wants comparison; *"…vs. …"* or *"differences between…"* usually wants contrast; *"Compare and contrast…"* or a *(Compare/Contrast)* label with no direction can take either, and the safest plan is **both**.

## Structure and outline template

### Point-by-point organisation

The slides organise every version **point by point**: each body paragraph takes **one point of comparison** (a basis such as *flexibility*) and discusses **both subjects** in that paragraph.

| Paragraph | Content (slide 4.2: contrast) |
|---|---|
| Introduction | Background (education has changed with technology), thesis listing three bases |
| Body 1 | **Interaction:** offline face-to-face vs. online through devices |
| Body 2 | **Flexibility:** fixed time and place vs. study from anywhere |
| Body 3 | **Learning environment:** structured classroom vs. home with distractions |
| Conclusion | Both have value, but they differ in the three bases |

This is clearer than writing everything about one subject and then everything about the other, because the reader sees each difference directly.

### When you cover both similarities and differences

The full slide (4) uses **five body paragraphs**: two similarities, then three differences. In the exam's three-paragraph outline, a strong plan is:

| Paragraph | Content |
|---|---|
| Body 1 | Similarities (one or two, briefly) |
| Body 2 | Difference 1 |
| Body 3 | Difference 2 (or differences 2 and 3) |

The thesis signals this balance with **Although…**: *Although [A] and [B] share some similarities, they differ significantly in terms of [x], [y] and [z].*

### Transitions for comparing and contrasting

| Similarity | Difference |
|---|---|
| similarly, likewise, in the same way, both… and…, also, just as… so too… | however, in contrast, on the other hand, whereas, while, unlike, although, but |

## Writing procedure

1. **Identify the focus**: similarities, differences or both.
2. **List the bases of comparison** that apply to both subjects (cost, convenience, environment, interaction, quality…).
3. **Choose three bases** that give the clearest contrast (or likeness).
4. **Write the thesis** naming the bases, with *Although…* if you include both similarities and differences.
5. **Complete the outline**, with one basis per topic sentence.
6. **Write each body paragraph point by point**: state the basis → subject A → transition (*In contrast,…*) → subject B → result or comment.
7. **Conclude** by restating the main similarity or difference and, if natural, saying which option suits whom.

## Model essays

Two complete models: one with similarities **and** differences (like slide 4) and one contrast-only (like slide 4.2).

:::model Model essay 1 · Similarities and differences | City Life and Village Life
--- outline
- **Topic:** City Life and Village Life
- **Thesis Statement:** Although city life and village life share some important values, they differ significantly in terms of opportunities, cost of living and environment.
- **Supporting Points/Plan of Development:** i) Similarity: family ties and community values ii) Difference: opportunities iii) Difference: cost of living and environment
- **Topic sentence of the first supporting paragraph:** One important similarity between city life and village life is the strong value people place on family and community.
- **Topic sentence of the second supporting paragraph:** However, cities and villages differ greatly in the opportunities they offer.
- **Topic sentence of the third supporting paragraph:** City life and village life also differ in their cost of living and their environment.
- **Concluding sentence:** Ultimately, both ways of life have their own strengths, and the better choice depends on what a person values most.
--- answer Essay
Every year, before Eid, millions of people leave Dhaka and travel to their home villages, filling buses, trains and launches. This great journey shows that most people in Bangladesh live with a foot in two worlds: the busy city where they work and the quiet village where their roots lie. As cities grow rapidly, more and more people are choosing between these two ways of life. Each has its own attractions and difficulties. Although city life and village life share some important values, they differ significantly in terms of opportunities, cost of living and environment.

One important similarity between city life and village life is the strong value people place on family and community. In both places, family remains the centre of life. Villagers often live in joint families, while many city families support parents and relatives and visit them regularly. Similarly, both cities and villages have strong community traditions. Neighbours in a village help each other during harvests and weddings, and in the same way, residents of a city apartment building or neighbourhood celebrate festivals together and help one another in emergencies. Thus, wherever they live, people continue to depend on family and community.

However, cities and villages differ greatly in the opportunities they offer. Cities provide access to universities, specialist hospitals, large companies and a wide range of jobs, so young people move there to study and build careers. In contrast, villages usually have fewer schools of high quality, limited health care and work that depends mainly on farming, fishing and small businesses. For example, a student who wants to study engineering or medicine often has to move to a city. As a result, cities attract ambitious young people, while many villages lose their educated youth.

City life and village life also differ in their cost of living and their environment. Life in the city is expensive: rent, transport, food and education cost far more than in a village. On the other hand, villages offer a lower cost of living, because people often own their homes and grow some of their own food. The environment is also very different. Cities suffer from traffic jams, noise, air pollution and crowded housing, whereas villages offer open fields, fresh air, quiet nights and closeness to nature. Therefore, although cities offer more opportunities, villages often provide a healthier and calmer life.

In conclusion, city life and village life are similar in their strong family and community bonds, but they differ clearly in opportunities, expenses and surroundings. The city rewards ambition with education and careers, while the village offers affordability, peace and a cleaner environment. Ultimately, both ways of life have their own strengths, and the better choice depends on what a person values most.
--- note
Like slide 4, the thesis begins with **Although** to signal similarities *and* differences. The similarity paragraph comes **first**, followed by the differences, which carry the main weight. Each difference paragraph is **point by point**: city → *In contrast / On the other hand / whereas* → village → result. Compare-and-contrast transitions (*Similarly, However, In contrast, whereas*) make the relationships explicit.
:::

:::model Model essay 2 · Contrast only | Printed Books vs. E-books
--- outline
- **Topic:** Printed Books vs. E-books
- **Thesis Statement:** Printed books and e-books differ significantly in terms of reading experience, convenience and cost.
- **Supporting Points/Plan of Development:** i) Reading experience ii) Convenience iii) Cost
- **Topic sentence of the first supporting paragraph:** One major difference between printed books and e-books is the reading experience they offer.
- **Topic sentence of the second supporting paragraph:** Another important difference is convenience.
- **Topic sentence of the third supporting paragraph:** Printed books and e-books also differ in cost.
- **Concluding sentence:** Ultimately, both formats can support a love of reading, but they differ in experience, convenience and cost.
--- answer Essay
For centuries, the printed book was the only way to read a story or study a subject. Today, however, a whole library can fit inside a mobile phone. E-books, which can be read on phones, tablets and e-readers, have become increasingly popular among students and professionals. Yet many readers still prefer the feel of paper in their hands. Although both formats allow people to enjoy reading and gain knowledge, they offer very different experiences. Printed books and e-books differ significantly in terms of reading experience, convenience and cost.

One major difference between printed books and e-books is the reading experience they offer. A printed book can be touched, smelled and held; readers can turn its pages, write notes in the margins and see at a glance how much they have read. Many people also find it easier to concentrate on paper and remember what they have read. In contrast, e-books are read on a glowing screen, which can strain the eyes during long reading sessions. However, e-books allow readers to adjust the font size and brightness, which is helpful for people with weak eyesight. Therefore, printed books offer a more natural experience, while e-books offer more control.

Another important difference is convenience. Printed books are heavy and take up space, so a student cannot easily carry ten textbooks at once. E-books, on the other hand, are extremely portable: hundreds of them can be stored on a single device and carried anywhere. They can also be bought and downloaded instantly, and readers can search for a word or topic in seconds. Printed books, however, never need charging and are not affected by technical problems or a weak internet connection. As a result, e-books are more convenient for travel and research, whereas printed books are more reliable.

Printed books and e-books also differ in cost. Printed books require paper, printing and transport, so they are often expensive, especially imported academic textbooks. E-books are usually cheaper, and many classic works are available free of charge. However, reading e-books requires a device such as a smartphone or tablet, which can be costly. Printed books can also be shared, lent, borrowed from libraries and resold, while e-books are often tied to one account. Thus, e-books are cheaper per book, but printed books can be shared more freely.

In conclusion, printed books provide a natural, reliable and shareable reading experience, while e-books offer portability, searchability and lower prices. Neither format is perfect for everyone: a student preparing for an exam may prefer paper, while a traveller may prefer a device. Ultimately, both formats can support a love of reading, but they differ in experience, convenience and cost.
--- note
A **contrast-only** essay like slide 4.2, with three **bases of contrast** named in the thesis and used as the topic sentences in the same order. Each paragraph discusses **both subjects** and ends with a sentence that sums up the difference (*Therefore, printed books offer a more natural experience, while e-books offer more control*). The essay stays balanced: each format wins on some points.
:::

## Examination strategy

- **Interpret the prompt.** Decide whether the topic wants similarities, differences or both. When the label is just *(Compare/Contrast)*, use the slides' **Although…** thesis and cover both.
- **Organise quickly.** Make a three-column table: *Basis | Subject A | Subject B*. Fill four or five rows, then choose the three most interesting bases.
- **Choose supporting points.** A basis must apply to **both** subjects. Do not discuss the cost of one and the location of the other.
- **Avoid common errors:** describing the two subjects separately with no comparison, unbalanced treatment (four sentences on A, one on B), bases that overlap, and forgetting the comparing and contrasting transitions.
- **Time:** outline 5 minutes, essay 30 to 35 minutes, check 3 minutes.
- **Unfamiliar pair?** Useful bases for almost any pair: **cost, convenience, quality, effect on health, effect on relationships, environment, opportunities**.

:::practice Practice 3 · Body paragraph | Write one point-by-point paragraph
Write one body paragraph (100 to 130 words) contrasting **public universities and private universities** on the basis of **cost**.
--- skill
Point-by-point development with contrast transitions
--- answer
One major difference between public and private universities is their cost. Public universities are funded largely by the government, so their tuition fees are very low, and many students pay only a small amount each year. As a result, talented students from poor families can receive higher education without heavy financial pressure. In contrast, private universities depend mainly on student fees, so a degree can cost several lakh taka. Students and their families often have to save for years or take loans to pay these fees. However, many private universities offer scholarships and waivers based on results, which reduces the burden for strong students. Therefore, public universities are far more affordable, while private universities require greater financial planning.
