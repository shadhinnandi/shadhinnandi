# Cause and Effect Essay

## Definition and purpose

A cause and effect essay **explains why something happens and/or what happens as a result**. It is the essay version of the cause-and-effect paragraph from the midterm. Its purpose is to show clear, logical connections, not just to list reasons.

The slides model one subject, *Natural Disasters*, in **three versions**. Together they show the three possible focuses:

| Slide | Focus | Thesis |
|---|---|---|
| 2.1 Cause essay | **Causes only** | *Natural disasters are caused mainly by natural geological and climatic processes, as well as human activities that can intensify their occurrence and severity.* |
| 2.2 Effect essay | **Effects only** | *Natural disasters have devastating effects on human life, property and the economy, and the environment and society.* |
| 2. Cause and effect essay | **Both** | *Natural disasters are caused by geological and climatic processes as well as human activities, and they result in serious consequences for human life, the economy, and the environment.* |

The topic tells you the focus. *"Causes of…"* wants three causes; *"Effects of…"* wants three effects; *"Causes and effects of…"* or a topic with no direction (*"Plastic Pollution in Rivers (Cause and Effect)"*) wants both.

## Structure and outline template

### Causes only or effects only

| Paragraph | Content |
|---|---|
| Introduction | Hook, general statement, background, thesis naming three causes (or effects) |
| Body 1 | Cause/effect 1, explained with examples |
| Body 2 | Cause/effect 2 |
| Body 3 | Cause/effect 3 |
| Conclusion | Restate, summarise, final thought (often why understanding the issue matters) |

The slides' cause essay groups causes into **geological**, **climatic** and **human**. The effect essay groups effects into **loss of life and injuries**, **damage to property and the economy**, and **environmental and social consequences**. Grouping is the key skill: many small causes become three clear categories.

### Causes and effects together

The slides' combined essay uses **two cause paragraphs and one effect paragraph**:

| Plan of development (slides) | Paragraph |
|---|---|
| i) Natural and geological causes | Body 1: cause |
| ii) Human and climatic causes | Body 2: cause |
| iii) Major effects of natural disasters | Body 3: effects |

You may also use one cause paragraph and two effect paragraphs if the topic is more about results. Either way, keep **three body paragraphs** to match the exam outline.

### Inside each body paragraph

Use the midterm pattern: **cause → how it happens → example → effect**, with signal words (*because, due to, as a result, consequently, therefore, leads to*).

## Writing procedure

1. **Identify the focus** from the topic: causes, effects or both.
2. **Brainstorm** every cause and effect you can think of (2 minutes).
3. **Group** them into three categories (for "both", two cause groups and one effect group).
4. **Order** them logically: natural before human, immediate before long-term, or least to most important.
5. **Write the thesis** naming the three groups.
6. **Complete the outline**, then write: introduction, three body paragraphs, conclusion.
7. **Check** that every cause really leads to the result and every link is signalled.

## Model essays

Two complete models: one covering both causes and effects (like the slides' combined essay) and one on effects only.

:::model Model essay 1 · Causes and effects | Causes and Effects of Deforestation in Bangladesh
--- outline
- **Topic:** Causes and Effects of Deforestation in Bangladesh
- **Thesis Statement:** Deforestation in Bangladesh is caused mainly by population pressure and economic activities, and it results in serious damage to the environment and to people's lives.
- **Supporting Points/Plan of Development:** i) Population pressure ii) Economic activities iii) Effects on the environment and people
- **Topic sentence of the first supporting paragraph:** The growing population of Bangladesh is a major cause of deforestation.
- **Topic sentence of the second supporting paragraph:** Economic activities such as illegal logging, brick kilns and commercial farming also destroy large areas of forest.
- **Topic sentence of the third supporting paragraph:** The loss of forests has severe effects on the environment and on human lives.
- **Concluding sentence:** Thus, deforestation has clear human causes and dangerous effects, which makes forest protection an urgent national responsibility.
--- answer Essay
Forests are often called the lungs of the earth, yet in Bangladesh they are disappearing at an alarming rate. A healthy country needs a large area of forest to keep its air clean, protect its soil and support wildlife. However, Bangladesh has far less forest cover than it needs, and areas such as the hills of Chattogram and the sal forests of the central region have shrunk dramatically over the past decades. Deforestation in Bangladesh is caused mainly by population pressure and economic activities, and it results in serious damage to the environment and to people's lives.

The growing population of Bangladesh is a major cause of deforestation. As one of the most densely populated countries in the world, Bangladesh constantly needs more land for housing, farming, roads and industries. Consequently, forest land is cleared to build new settlements and to grow crops for an increasing number of people. In addition, many poor rural families depend on wood for cooking fuel because they cannot afford gas. As a result, trees are cut every day, often faster than new ones can grow. This pressure is especially strong near forests where people have few other sources of income.

Economic activities such as illegal logging, brick kilns and commercial farming also destroy large areas of forest. Valuable trees are cut illegally and sold to timber traders, often with the help of corruption. Many brick kilns burn wood as fuel, so they create a constant demand for timber. Moreover, natural forests are sometimes replaced by commercial plantations or tobacco farms that bring quick profit but do not support wildlife. Because laws against these activities are not strictly enforced, the destruction continues year after year.

The loss of forests has severe effects on the environment and on human lives. Without tree roots to hold the soil, hillsides become unstable, and heavy monsoon rain can cause deadly landslides, as has happened repeatedly in the Chattogram hill areas. Deforestation also destroys the homes of animals such as elephants, deer and many birds, pushing some species towards extinction. Furthermore, fewer trees mean less carbon dioxide is absorbed, which contributes to climate change and hotter temperatures. Finally, communities that depend on forests for food, medicine and income lose their livelihoods and are often forced to migrate to cities.

In conclusion, deforestation in Bangladesh is driven by the needs of a growing population and by profitable but harmful economic activities. Its effects, from landslides and lost wildlife to climate change and poverty, threaten both nature and people. Thus, deforestation has clear human causes and dangerous effects, which makes forest protection an urgent national responsibility.
--- note
This follows the slides' combined essay: **two cause paragraphs, one effect paragraph**. Many small causes are **grouped** into two categories (population pressure, economic activities). Each paragraph uses the **cause → how → example → effect** pattern and signal words (*Consequently, As a result, Because, Furthermore*). The effects move from **immediate** (landslides) to **long-term** (climate change, migration).
:::

:::model Model essay 2 · Effects only | Effects of Social Media on University Students
--- outline
- **Topic:** Effects of Social Media on University Students
- **Thesis Statement:** Social media has significant effects on university students' academic life, their mental health and their social relationships.
- **Supporting Points/Plan of Development:** i) Effects on academic life ii) Effects on mental health iii) Effects on social relationships
- **Topic sentence of the first supporting paragraph:** Social media affects students' academic life in both helpful and harmful ways.
- **Topic sentence of the second supporting paragraph:** Excessive use of social media can also harm students' mental health.
- **Topic sentence of the third supporting paragraph:** Social media has changed the way students build and maintain relationships.
- **Concluding sentence:** Therefore, the effects of social media depend largely on how wisely students use it.
--- answer Essay
For most university students, the first thing they check in the morning and the last thing they see at night is a social media feed. Platforms such as Facebook, Instagram, YouTube and TikTok have become a central part of student life. Students use them to stay in touch with friends, follow news, join study groups and share their own ideas. However, the hours spent on these platforms inevitably shape their lives in many ways. Social media has significant effects on university students' academic life, their mental health and their social relationships.

Social media affects students' academic life in both helpful and harmful ways. On the positive side, class groups on messaging apps allow students to share notes, assignment deadlines and lecture recordings instantly, and educational videos can explain difficult topics clearly. On the other hand, constant notifications break students' concentration. A student who checks the phone every few minutes while studying needs much longer to finish a task and remembers less of it. As a result, many students stay awake late into the night to finish work they could have completed earlier, and their performance in examinations suffers.

Excessive use of social media can also harm students' mental health. Students often compare their own lives with the carefully edited photographs and success stories of others, and this comparison can make them feel inadequate and anxious. Furthermore, cyberbullying and negative comments can cause serious emotional pain. Late-night scrolling also reduces sleep, which leads to tiredness, irritability and stress. Consequently, a platform designed to connect people can sometimes leave students feeling more lonely and worried than before.

Social media has changed the way students build and maintain relationships. It allows them to stay in contact with school friends who live in other cities, find people with similar interests and organise events quickly. For example, a university club can announce a programme to hundreds of students in minutes. However, online interaction sometimes replaces real conversation. Friends may sit together at a table while each one looks at a phone. Over time, this habit can weaken face-to-face communication skills, which are essential for interviews, teamwork and professional life.

In conclusion, social media influences students' studies, their emotional well-being and their friendships. It offers valuable tools for learning and connection, but uncontrolled use leads to distraction, anxiety and weaker personal relationships. Therefore, the effects of social media depend largely on how wisely students use it.
--- note
An **effects-only** essay, like slide 2.2. The three effects are grouped into broad **areas of life** (academic, mental, social), a strong way to organise effects. The first and third body paragraphs show **positive and negative** effects, which makes the essay balanced and realistic. The conclusion draws a lesson without adding new effects.
:::

## Examination strategy

- **Interpret the prompt.** Underline *causes*, *effects* or both. If the label says *(Cause and Effect)* without direction, write both, using the slides' 2 + 1 plan.
- **Organise quickly.** Make a two-column list (causes | effects), group the items, then choose your three paragraphs.
- **Choose supporting points.** Prefer causes and effects you can explain with an example from Bangladesh or student life.
- **Avoid common errors:** confusing a cause with an effect, listing causes without explaining *how* they cause the result, mixing causes and effects inside one paragraph, and writing a conclusion that introduces a new cause.
- **Time:** outline 5 minutes, essay 30 to 35 minutes, check 3 minutes.
- **Unfamiliar topic?** Group causes as **personal / social / economic** or **natural / human**; group effects as **personal / academic or economic / social or environmental**. These frames fit nearly any topic.

Matching the thesis to the focus, as in slides 2, 2.1 and 2.2

:::practice Practice 3 · Body paragraph | Develop one cause
Write one body paragraph (100 to 130 words) explaining **air pollution from vehicles** as a cause of health problems in cities. Use the pattern cause → how → example → effect.
--- skill
Developing a cause with explanation, example and effect
--- answer
One major cause of health problems in cities is air pollution from vehicles. Thousands of old buses, trucks and motorcycles on city roads burn fuel inefficiently and release thick smoke containing harmful gases and fine dust. Because traffic moves slowly, these vehicles stand in one place for long periods and fill the streets with exhaust. For example, people waiting at a busy intersection during rush hour breathe this polluted air directly. As a result, many city residents, especially children and elderly people, suffer from coughing, asthma and other breathing problems. Over time, such pollution can even contribute to heart disease.
:::

:::practice Practice 4 · Exam level | Complete essay
Write a complete cause and effect essay on **"Causes and Effects of Brain Drain from Bangladesh"**. Write the outline first, then the essay (about 400 to 500 words).
--- skill
The full 10-mark essay task
--- hint
Use the slides' 2 + 1 plan: causes (better pay and opportunities abroad; limited research facilities and jobs at home) and effects (loss of skilled people, but also money sent home).
--- answer
**Topic:** Causes and Effects of Brain Drain from Bangladesh
**Thesis Statement:** Brain drain from Bangladesh is caused by better opportunities abroad and limited facilities at home, and it has serious effects on the country's development.
**Supporting Points/Plan of Development:** i) Better opportunities abroad ii) Limited facilities and jobs at home iii) Effects on national development
**Topic sentence 1:** The attraction of better salaries, education and living standards abroad is a major cause of brain drain.
**Topic sentence 2:** At the same time, limited jobs, research facilities and career growth at home push talented people to leave.
**Topic sentence 3:** Brain drain has significant effects on the development of Bangladesh.
**Concluding sentence:** Therefore, unless better opportunities are created at home, Bangladesh will continue to lose many of its brightest minds.

*Body 3 should mention both the loss (fewer doctors, engineers and researchers; public money spent on their education benefits other countries) and a balancing point (remittances and knowledge sent back by those abroad).*
