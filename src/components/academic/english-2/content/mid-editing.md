# Editing and Proofreading

## What editing is

The slides define it: *editing is a process of writing in which the writer or editor rereads the draft, identifies the errors and corrects it.* In short:

> **Editing = improving grammar, sentence structure, punctuation, word choice and clarity.**

Editing is tested directly in both exams. The midterm gives a short paragraph with **four** numbered mistakes (2 marks); the final gives **eight** (4 marks). A box lists the error categories, and you must **identify, write and correct** each mistake.

## How to write the answer

Write one line per numbered mistake: the number, the category letter and name from the box, and the correction. This is the format used in the midterm answer key:

```text
1. d. irregular verb form: Mark went
2. b. run-on: very crowded; he had to wait   (or: very crowded. He had to wait)
```

Write the corrected words, not just the category. Half of each mark is for naming the error and half is for fixing it, so do both.

## The 14 error types

The slides cover fourteen kinds of error. The first nine are the categories that appeared in the midterm box.

### 1. Fragment

An incomplete sentence: it is missing a subject, a verb or a complete thought.

| Wrong | Correct |
|---|---|
| As I was tired. | As I was tired, I went to bed. |
| Standing right in the middle of the room. | The T-Rex was standing right in the middle of the room. |

**How to identify:** ask, *"Does this group of words express a complete thought?"* Watch for groups starting with *because, as, when, although, if* or an *-ing* word that stand alone.

### 2. Run-on sentence

Two or more complete sentences joined without proper punctuation or a conjunction.

| Wrong | Correct options |
|---|---|
| The museum was crowded he had to wait outside. | The museum was crowded. He had to wait outside. · The museum was crowded, so he had to wait outside. · The museum was crowded; he had to wait outside. |

**How to identify:** look for two complete sentences joined incorrectly. A comma alone between two sentences ("comma splice") is also a run-on.

### 3. Inconsistent verb tense

The writer changes tense without a reason while talking about the same time.

| Wrong | Correct |
|---|---|
| Yesterday, Sarah went to the library and borrows a book. | Yesterday, Sarah went to the library and borrowed a book. |

**How to identify:** find the time of the passage (often from words like *yesterday*, *last Friday*), then check that every verb matches it.

### 4. Irregular verb form

Irregular verbs do not form their past tense with *-ed*.

| Wrong | Correct |
|---|---|
| He goed to school yesterday. | He went to school yesterday. |
| She buyed a new phone. | She bought a new phone. |

Common ones: go → went, buy → bought, say → said, see → saw, take → took, bring → brought, catch → caught, teach → taught, think → thought, write → wrote, run → ran, begin → began.

### 5. Subject-verb agreement

The verb must agree with its subject in number and person.

| Wrong | Correct |
|---|---|
| She go to university every day. | She goes to university every day. |
| The students is ready. | The students are ready. |
| The list of books are on the table. | The list of books is on the table. |

**Trap:** the subject is *list*, not *books*. Ignore the phrase between the subject and the verb (*of books*, *of tickets and lunch*).

### 6. Missing comma

A comma is needed after an introductory phrase or clause, and between items in a series.

| Wrong | Correct |
|---|---|
| After finishing the exam Sara went home. | After finishing the exam, Sara went home. |
| She bought books pens notebooks and pencils. | She bought books, pens, notebooks and pencils. |

The slides note that the comma before *and* in a list is optional; both styles are correct.

### 7. Missing quotation marks

Quotation marks show someone's exact words.

| Wrong | Correct |
|---|---|
| The teacher said, You should submit your assignment tomorrow. | The teacher said, "You should submit your assignment tomorrow." |

**How to identify:** look for direct speech after *said, told, asked, shouted*.

### 8. Missing article

Articles are *a, an* and *the*.

| Rule | Wrong | Correct |
|---|---|---|
| *a* before a singular countable noun with a consonant sound | She bought book. | She bought a book. |
| *an* before a vowel sound | She is a engineer. | She is an engineer. |
| *the* for something specific or already mentioned | I saw a movie yesterday. A movie was excellent. | I saw a movie yesterday. The movie was excellent. |

Ask: *"Is a singular countable noun missing an article?"* Remember it is the **sound** that matters: *an hour*, *a university*.

### 9. Apostrophe mistake

Apostrophes show possession or form contractions.

| Use | Wrong | Correct |
|---|---|---|
| Singular possession | This is Rahim book. | This is Rahim's book. |
| Plural possession | The students books are on the table. | The students' books are on the table. |
| Contraction | I dont know. | I don't know. |
| it's / its | Its raining. | It's raining. |

*It's* = it is. *Its* = possession (*The dog wagged its tail*).

### 10. Capitalisation

| Rule | Wrong | Correct |
|---|---|---|
| Beginning of a sentence | yesterday, I went to school. | Yesterday, I went to school. |
| Proper nouns | I live in bangladesh. | I live in Bangladesh. |
| Languages and nationalities | She speaks english. | She speaks English. |
| Days and months | We have a test on monday. My birthday is in november. | We have a test on Monday. My birthday is in November. |

### 11. Homonyms (commonly confused words)

| Words | Meaning | Wrong → correct |
|---|---|---|
| their / there / they're | possession / place or existence / they are | Their going to the library. → They're going to the library. |
| your / you're | possession / you are | Your very kind. → You're very kind. |
| to / too / two | direction or infinitive / also or excessively / number | I want too go home. → I want to go home. |
| its / it's | possession / it is | |

### 12. Pronoun error

| Problem | Wrong | Correct |
|---|---|---|
| Subject/object confusion | Me and Rafi went to the library. | Rafi and I went to the library. |
| Agreement (traditional formal grammar) | Every student must submit their assignment. | Every student must submit his or her assignment. |

The slides add that the singular *they* (*Every student must submit their assignment*) is widely accepted in contemporary English. Use *his or her* if your teacher marks by traditional rules. The slides also teach the **3-2-1 order** for polite pronoun lists: third person, then second, then first (*he, you and I*).

### 13. Preposition error

Some word + preposition combinations must be learned.

| Wrong | Correct |
|---|---|
| She is interested on linguistics. | She is interested in linguistics. |
| It depends of the situation. | It depends on the situation. |
| She arrived to the university. | She arrived at the university. |

Others to learn: *good at, afraid of, proud of, responsible for, married to, different from, angry with (a person), angry about (a thing)*.

### 14. Spelling

| Wrong | Correct |
|---|---|
| recive | receive |
| seperate | separate |
| accomodation | accommodation |
| definately | definitely |

> **Correction to the slides:** slide 20 lists the correct form of *accomodation* as "accomodation". The correct spelling has double *c* and double *m*: **accommodation**.

### Also tested in the final: faulty parallelism

The final paper adds **faulty parallelism**: items in a list or pair must have the same grammatical form.

| Wrong | Correct |
|---|---|
| The trip included visiting a museum, exploring a park, and to eat lunch. | The trip included visiting a museum, exploring a park, and eating lunch. |

## Step-by-step method in the exam

1. **Read the whole paragraph once** to find its time (past or present) and its story.
2. **Go to each numbered word.** The number marks where the error is. Read the full sentence around it.
3. **Test the categories in this order:** Is the sentence complete? (fragment) Are two sentences joined? (run-on) Is the verb right? (tense, irregular form, agreement) Then punctuation (comma, apostrophe, quotation marks) and finally articles and other words.
4. **Write the answer line:** number, category, correction.
5. **Check the box:** each category usually appears once, but the box may contain extra categories that are not used.

## Common mistakes

- Naming the category but not writing the correction (half marks lost).
- Calling every error "fragment" or "run-on" without checking.
- Correcting words that are not numbered: only the numbered mistakes count.
- Confusing *inconsistent tense* (a correct verb form in the wrong tense, such as *copies* for *copied*) with *irregular verb form* (a verb form that does not exist, such as *goed*).

## Revision summary

> **Sentence problems:** fragment, run-on, faulty parallelism. **Verb problems:** inconsistent tense, irregular form, subject-verb agreement. **Punctuation:** comma, apostrophe, quotation marks, capitals. **Word problems:** article, homonym, pronoun, preposition, spelling.
>
> Answer format: *number · category · correction*.

## Practice

:::practice Practice 1 · Basic | Name the error
Name the error type in each sentence and correct it.

i. The children was playing in the field.
ii. Because the bus was late.
iii. My brother is a engineer.
iv. She teached us English last year.
v. I am good in mathematics.
--- skill
Recognising error categories one at a time
--- answer
i. **Subject-verb agreement:** The children **were** playing in the field.

ii. **Fragment:** Because the bus was late, **I missed the first class.**

iii. **Missing/incorrect article:** My brother is **an** engineer.

iv. **Irregular verb form:** She **taught** us English last year.

v. **Preposition error:** I am good **at** mathematics.
:::

The following paragraph contains four mistakes among these categories: **a.** fragment **b.** run-on **c.** inconsistent verb tense **d.** irregular verb form **e.** subject verb agreement **f.** missing comma **g.** missing quotation marks **h.** missing article **i.** apostrophe mistake. Identify, write and correct them.

Last Saturday, Nadia visited her grandparents in Cumilla. She ¹taked the early train because she wanted to arrive before lunch. --- skill
The midterm editing task: identify, name and correct four errors
--- hint
Check the time of the passage (*Last Saturday*) for errors 1 and 2. Look for direct speech in 3 and possession in 4.
--- answer
1. **d. irregular verb form:** She **took** the early train.
2. **c. inconsistent verb tense:** Her grandmother **cooked** her favourite fish curry.
3. **g. missing quotation marks:** her grandfather said, **"Welcome home, my dear."**
4. **i. apostrophe mistake:** in her **grandparents'** garden *(plural possession: the garden belongs to both grandparents)*.

Find and correct the five mistakes. Name each category.

The library ¹are open until nine o'clock on weekdays. Many students study there in the evening ²it is quiet and comfortable. Yesterday, I ³meet my friend Arif there. ⁴After we finished our assignment we went to the cafeteria. The coffee is cheap and delicious, but ⁵its usually crowded.

Applying the step-by-step method to several error types

1. **Subject-verb agreement:** The library **is** open.
2. **Run-on:** in the evening**, because** it is quiet and comfortable. *(Or: in the evening. It is quiet and comfortable.)*
