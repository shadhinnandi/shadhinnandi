# Essay Structure and Outline

## What an essay is

An essay is a group of paragraphs that develop **one main idea**, the thesis. If a paragraph explains one point in about 150 to 200 words, an essay explains a larger point in about 400 to 600 words by giving each supporting point a full paragraph of its own.

| Paragraph | Essay |
|---|---|
| Topic sentence | Introduction ending in a **thesis statement** |
| Three supporting points | Three **body paragraphs**, each with its own topic sentence |
| Concluding sentence | **Concluding paragraph** |

The final slides use the classic **five-paragraph essay**: one introduction, three body paragraphs and one conclusion. (An argumentative essay adds a refutation paragraph; a compare-and-contrast essay may have more body paragraphs. Those lessons explain how.)

## The parts of an essay

### 1. The introduction

The slides' model *A Rainy Day* labels the four parts of an introduction:

| Part | Job | From the slides |
|---|---|---|
| **Hook** | Catches the reader's interest | *The sky suddenly turns dark, and the first drops of rain begin to fall on the thirsty earth.* |
| **General statement** | Introduces the broad topic | *Rainy days are a common and beautiful part of life in Bangladesh.* |
| **Background** | Gives context and narrows towards the thesis | *They transform the familiar surroundings into a refreshing scene filled with cool air, wet streets, green trees…* |
| **Thesis statement** | States the main idea and the three points, usually as the last sentence | *A rainy day can be described through its beautiful surroundings, soothing sounds, and the different activities of people during the rain.* |

The introduction moves from **general to specific**, like a funnel, and ends with the thesis.

**Types of hook:** a vivid scene (as above), a surprising fact, a short question (*Have you ever…?*), a brief anecdote or a strong statement.

### 2. The body paragraphs

Each body paragraph develops **one** supporting point from the thesis, **in the same order** as the thesis lists them. Each body paragraph is built exactly like a midterm paragraph:

- **Topic sentence** stating the point (the slides write these in the outline).
- **Supporting details:** explanation, examples, facts, description.
- **A closing sentence** that ends the paragraph or links to the next (*Thus, a rainy day creates different experiences for different people*).

Use transitions **between** paragraphs (*Firstly, Another important…, Finally*) as well as within them.

### 3. The conclusion

The conclusion closes the essay. It:

1. **Restates the thesis in new words** (do not copy it exactly).
2. **Summarises the three supporting points** briefly.
3. **Ends with a final thought:** a reflection, a recommendation or the wider importance of the topic.

It does **not** introduce new points. The slides' conclusion to *A Rainy Day* ends: *For many people, a rainy day is not merely a change in weather but a beautiful experience filled with freshness, peace, and life.*

## The outline: the slides' format

Every final slide on essays begins with the same outline, and the final paper prints it as the *Sample Essay Structure*. You must write it before the essay (2 marks).

```text
Topic:
Thesis Statement:
Supporting Points/Plan of Development:
  i)
  ii)
  iii)
Topic sentence of the first supporting paragraph:
Topic sentence of the second supporting paragraph:
Topic sentence of the third supporting paragraph:
Concluding sentence:
```

### The slides' outline for *A Rainy Day*

| Outline part | Text |
|---|---|
| Topic | A Rainy Day |
| Thesis statement | A rainy day can be described through its beautiful surroundings, soothing sounds and the different activities of people during the rain. |
| Plan of development | i) Beautiful surroundings · ii) Soothing sounds · iii) Activities of people |
| Topic sentence 1 | A rainy day changes the appearance of the entire surroundings and gives nature a fresh, vibrant look. |
| Topic sentence 2 | The sounds of a rainy day create a peaceful and relaxing atmosphere. |
| Topic sentence 3 | A rainy day also changes the way people carry out their daily activities. |
| Concluding sentence | For many people, a rainy day is not merely a change in weather but a beautiful experience filled with freshness, peace, and life. |

Notice how the parts match: the **three items in the thesis** are the **plan of development**, and each one becomes the **topic sentence of one body paragraph**, in the same order. That alignment is what examiners check.

## How to interpret an essay prompt

The final offers three topics, each labelled with its type, for example *(Cause and Effect)*, *(Compare/Contrast)* or *(Argumentative)*.

1. **Read the label.** It tells you which structure to use. Writing a description for an argumentative topic loses marks even if the English is good.
2. **Find the key words.** *Causes* only? *Effects* only? *Should…?* means you must take a position. *vs.* means compare or contrast.
3. **Choose the topic you can support with three clear points.** Not the one that sounds most impressive.

## Coherence: making the essay flow

- **Order the body paragraphs logically:** importance (least to most), time, or a natural grouping (causes before effects).
- **Repeat key words** from the thesis in the topic sentences so the reader sees the connection.
- **Use transitions between paragraphs:** *Firstly / Secondly / Finally*, *Another important cause*, *However*, *In contrast*.
- **Keep every sentence on the point** of its paragraph, and every paragraph on the thesis.

## Step-by-step method in the exam

1. **Choose the topic** and note its type (1 minute).
2. **Brainstorm** five or six ideas; choose the best three (2 minutes).
3. **Write the thesis** with the three points in parallel form (1 minute).
4. **Complete the outline** in the slides' format (3 minutes).
5. **Write the introduction:** hook, general statement, background, thesis (5 minutes).
6. **Write three body paragraphs**, following your topic sentences (20 minutes).
7. **Write the conclusion:** restated thesis, summary, final thought (4 minutes).
8. **Check** the essay matches the outline, and edit for the editing-lesson errors (3 minutes).

## Checking your essay

- Does the thesis state the main point and list three points?
- Does each body paragraph match one point, in the same order?
- Does each body paragraph start with a clear topic sentence?
- Are there examples and explanations, not just statements?
- Does the conclusion restate the thesis without copying it, and add no new point?
- Did you check verbs, articles, commas and run-ons?

## Revision summary

> **Introduction:** hook → general statement → background → **thesis** (main idea + 3 points).
> **Body 1, 2, 3:** topic sentence (one point each, in thesis order) → explanation → examples → closing sentence.
> **Conclusion:** restate thesis → summarise points → final thought.
>
> Outline first (2 marks), then essay (8 marks). The thesis, plan of development and topic sentences must match.

:::practice Practice 2 · Outline preparation | Match the thesis and the topic sentences
**Thesis:** Learning to cook benefits young people because it improves their health, saves money and builds independence.

Which topic sentence does *not* belong in this essay, and why?

a) Firstly, cooking at home allows young people to choose healthier ingredients.
b) Secondly, home-cooked meals cost far less than eating at restaurants.
c) Thirdly, fast-food restaurants are becoming more popular in cities.
d) Finally, cooking teaches young people to look after themselves.
--- skill
Checking alignment between the thesis and the body paragraphs
--- answer
**c)** It is not one of the thesis points (health, money, independence), and it is about restaurants, not about the benefits of cooking. The correct order is a, b, d, matching the thesis.
:::

:::practice Practice 3 · Exam level | Write a complete outline
Write a complete outline in the slides' format for a descriptive essay on **"A Visit to a Historical Place"**.
--- skill
The 2-mark essay outline
--- hint
Pick a real place (Lalbagh Fort, Sonargaon, Paharpur). Three aspects: its history, its architecture, the atmosphere or visitors.
--- answer
**Topic:** A Visit to a Historical Place: Lalbagh Fort
**Thesis Statement:** My visit to Lalbagh Fort can be described through its rich history, its striking Mughal architecture and the peaceful atmosphere of its gardens.
**Supporting Points/Plan of Development:** i) Rich history ii) Mughal architecture iii) Peaceful gardens
**Topic sentence of the first supporting paragraph:** Lalbagh Fort carries a long and fascinating history that dates back to the Mughal period.
**Topic sentence of the second supporting paragraph:** The architecture of the fort shows the beauty and skill of Mughal builders.
**Topic sentence of the third supporting paragraph:** The green gardens of the fort create a calm atmosphere in the middle of busy Old Dhaka.
**Concluding sentence:** Thus, a visit to Lalbagh Fort is not only a trip into the past but also a peaceful escape from the noise of the modern city.
