# Cause and Effect Paragraph

## What it is

The slides define it: *a cause-and-effect paragraph is a paragraph that explains the reasons (causes) why something happens and/or the results (effects) that happen because of it. In other words, it shows the relationship between an event or situation and its causes or consequences.*

Features from the slides:

- Explains **why** something happens and/or **what happens as a result**.
- Shows a clear **cause-and-effect relationship**.
- Uses **logical reasoning and supporting examples**.
- Uses **signal words** such as *because, therefore, as a result, consequently*.
- Organises ideas in a **clear, logical order**.
- Ends with a **concluding statement**.

## Three ways to focus

| Focus | Question it answers | Example topic |
|---|---|---|
| **Causes only** | Why does it happen? | Causes of air pollution in cities |
| **Effects only** | What happens because of it? | Effects of regular exercise |
| **Causes and effects** | Why does it happen, and what follows? | Causes and effects of traffic congestion |

Read the topic carefully: *"causes of…"* wants reasons, *"effects of…"* wants results, and *"causes and effects of…"* wants both.

## The pattern from the slides

The slides give a pattern worth memorising:

> **Cause → Why/how it happens → Example → Effect**

### The slide example, analysed

The slides discuss **Causes and Effects of Sleep Deprivation among University Students**:

| Part | Content |
|---|---|
| Topic sentence | Introduces the topic and indicates that the paragraph will discuss causes and effects: *Sleep deprivation is a common problem among university students, and it can be caused by several factors and lead to various negative effects.* |
| Cause 1 | Smartphone/social media use → explanation (scrolling at night) → example → effect (fewer hours of sleep, tired next morning) |
| Cause 2 | Academic pressure → explanation (staying awake for assignments) → example → effect (difficulty concentrating) |
| Effect 1 | Poor academic performance → explanation → examples (remembering, attention, tasks) |
| Effect 2 | Stress and irritability → explanation → consequence (relationships suffer) |
| Concluding sentence | Summarises the main causes and effects |

## Signal words

| To show a cause | To show an effect |
|---|---|
| because, since, as, due to, because of, owing to, one reason is, is caused by, results from | therefore, as a result, consequently, so, thus, hence, leads to, results in, causes |

**Grammar note:** *because* and *since* are followed by a clause (*because the roads are narrow*); *due to* and *because of* are followed by a noun phrase (*because of narrow roads*).

## Step-by-step method

1. **Identify the focus**: causes, effects, or both.
2. **List the causes or effects** quickly, then choose the strongest three (or two causes and two effects for a "both" topic).
3. **Order them logically:** from most important to least, or from immediate to long-term.
4. **Develop each point with the slides' pattern:** cause → how it happens → example → effect.
5. **Use signal words** to make every relationship explicit.
6. **Conclude** by summarising the causes and effects, and, if natural, suggest why the issue matters.

## Revision summary

> **Topic sentence** (topic + causes/effects focus) → **point 1**: cause → how → example → effect → **point 2** → **point 3** → **concluding sentence** summarising the causes and/or effects.
>
> Check the focus of the topic. Signal every link: *because, due to, as a result, consequently, therefore*.

## Model paragraphs

Four models with different focuses: both, causes only, effects only, and both on an environmental issue.

:::model Model paragraph 1 · Causes and effects | Traffic Congestion in Dhaka
--- outline
- **Topic:** Causes and effects of traffic congestion in Dhaka
- **Topic sentence:** Traffic congestion in Dhaka has several clear causes, and it seriously affects the lives of city residents.
- **Supporting point 1:** Cause: too many vehicles on limited roads
- **Supporting point 2:** Cause: poor traffic management and unplanned growth
- **Supporting point 3:** Effects: lost time, economic loss and stress
- **Concluding sentence:** Therefore, too many vehicles and poor planning cause Dhaka's traffic jams, while lost time, money and health are their heavy price.
--- answer Paragraph
Traffic congestion in Dhaka has several clear causes, and it seriously affects the lives of city residents. One major cause is the huge number of vehicles on limited roads. Thousands of new cars, motorbikes and buses are registered every year, but the road space of the city has hardly increased. As a result, main roads such as Mirpur Road and the airport road are blocked for hours every day. Another cause is poor traffic management and unplanned growth. Many drivers ignore traffic signals, buses stop anywhere to pick up passengers, and shops and markets occupy the footpaths. Consequently, pedestrians walk on the road and slow the traffic further. These traffic jams have serious effects. Commuters lose two or three hours every day, so they have less time for work, study and family. Moreover, businesses lose money because goods are delivered late, and drivers burn extra fuel. Long hours in noisy, polluted traffic also cause stress, headaches and fatigue. Therefore, too many vehicles and poor planning cause Dhaka's traffic jams, while lost time, money and health are their heavy price.
--- note
The paragraph gives **two causes and then the effects**, like the slide model. Each cause follows **cause → how → example → effect** (*registered every year → road space hardly increased → Mirpur Road → blocked for hours*). Signal words make every link visible.
:::

:::model Model paragraph 2 · Causes only | Why Air Pollution Is Rising in Cities
--- outline
- **Topic:** Causes of air pollution in cities
- **Topic sentence:** Air pollution in the cities of Bangladesh is rising mainly because of vehicle emissions, brick kilns and construction work.
- **Supporting point 1:** Vehicle emissions
- **Supporting point 2:** Brick kilns around cities
- **Supporting point 3:** Uncontrolled construction dust
- **Concluding sentence:** Thus, vehicles, brick kilns and construction are the main reasons why city air is becoming more harmful every year.
--- answer Paragraph
Air pollution in the cities of Bangladesh is rising mainly because of vehicle emissions, brick kilns and construction work. The first cause is smoke from vehicles. Many buses and trucks on city roads are old and poorly maintained, so they release thick black smoke full of harmful gases. Because the number of vehicles keeps growing, the amount of smoke in the air increases every year. The second cause is the large number of brick kilns around the cities. Most of these kilns burn coal and sometimes even wood, and they operate for months during the dry season. As a result, winds carry their smoke and fine dust straight into nearby cities, especially in winter. The third cause is uncontrolled construction. New buildings, roads and flyovers are being built everywhere, but sand, cement and soil are often left uncovered. Consequently, dust rises into the air whenever vehicles pass or the wind blows. Thus, vehicles, brick kilns and construction are the main reasons why city air is becoming more harmful every year.
--- note
A **causes-only** topic: no effects are needed, so all three points are causes. Each one still ends with its **immediate result** (*the amount of smoke increases*), which keeps the reasoning logical.
:::

:::model Model paragraph 3 · Effects only | The Benefits of Regular Exercise for Students
--- outline
- **Topic:** Effects of regular exercise on students
- **Topic sentence:** Regular exercise has several positive effects on the lives of university students.
- **Supporting point 1:** Better physical health
- **Supporting point 2:** Better concentration and memory
- **Supporting point 3:** Lower stress and better mood
- **Concluding sentence:** In conclusion, regular exercise leads to a healthier body, a sharper mind and a happier student life.
--- answer Paragraph
Regular exercise has several positive effects on the lives of university students. First, it improves physical health. Students who walk, cycle or play sports for at least thirty minutes a day strengthen their heart and muscles and keep their weight under control. As a result, they fall ill less often and miss fewer classes. Second, exercise improves concentration and memory. Physical activity increases the flow of blood and oxygen to the brain, which helps students stay alert. For example, a short walk before studying can make it easier to focus on a difficult chapter. Consequently, students who exercise regularly often find that they learn more efficiently. Finally, exercise reduces stress and improves mood. University life brings deadlines, examinations and financial worries, but exercise releases chemicals in the brain that make people feel calmer and happier. Therefore, a game of football or badminton after classes can refresh students for the evening's work. In conclusion, regular exercise leads to a healthier body, a sharper mind and a happier student life.
--- note
An **effects-only** topic. The effects are arranged from **body to mind to emotions**, a logical order. Each effect is explained (*why* it happens: increased blood flow, released chemicals) before its result.
:::

:::model Model paragraph 4 · Causes and effects | River Erosion in Bangladesh
--- outline
- **Topic:** Causes and effects of river erosion in Bangladesh
- **Topic sentence:** River erosion is a serious problem in Bangladesh that results from both natural forces and human activities and has devastating effects on rural people.
- **Supporting point 1:** Natural causes: strong currents and floods
- **Supporting point 2:** Human causes: sand extraction and cutting trees
- **Supporting point 3:** Effects: loss of land, homes and livelihoods
- **Concluding sentence:** In short, natural forces and careless human activities cause river erosion, and thousands of families pay for it with their land and homes.
--- answer Paragraph
River erosion is a serious problem in Bangladesh that results from both natural forces and human activities and has devastating effects on rural people. The main natural cause is the strong current of great rivers such as the Padma, the Jamuna and the Meghna. During the monsoon, these rivers carry huge volumes of water, and the fast current cuts away the soft, sandy riverbanks. In addition, floods weaken the soil so that large pieces of land collapse into the river. Human activities make the problem worse. Illegal sand extraction from riverbeds changes the flow of water, and the cutting of trees along the banks removes the roots that hold the soil together. As a result, erosion becomes faster and harder to predict. The effects are tragic. Every year, thousands of families lose their homes, farmland and livestock overnight. Many of them become landless and move to urban slums in search of work, while children often have to leave school. In short, natural forces and careless human activities cause river erosion, and thousands of families pay for it with their land and homes.
--- note
Causes are grouped into **natural** and **human**, a clear way to organise a "both" topic in limited space. The effects move from **immediate** (homes lost overnight) to **long-term** (migration, children leaving school).
:::

## Exam strategy

- **Interpret the prompt.** Underline *causes*, *effects* or both. Writing effects for a causes-only topic is answering the wrong question.
- **Organise fast.** Draw a simple chain: *cause → result*. For a "both" topic, use two causes and one combined effects point (or one cause point and two effects points) so you still have three supporting points.
- **Choose relevant details.** Prefer causes and effects you can explain with an example. A realistic local example (Dhaka traffic, a riverbank) is better than a vague statement.
- **Avoid common errors:** confusing cause with effect, listing reasons without explaining *how* they cause the result, and using *because of* with a full clause (*because of the roads are narrow* ✗).
- **Time:** 3 minutes outline, 12 to 15 minutes writing, 2 minutes checking.
- **Unfamiliar topic?** Ask *why?* three times for causes and *so what?* three times for effects. You will have your points in a minute.

:::practice Practice 3 · Exam level | Outline and paragraph
Write a cause-and-effect paragraph of 150 to 200 words on **"Causes and effects of food adulteration"**. Show your outline in the exam format.
--- skill
The full midterm task for a cause-and-effect topic
--- hint
Causes: greed for profit, weak monitoring and punishment. Effects: serious diseases, loss of public trust. Use the pattern *cause → how → example → effect*.
--- answer
**Topic:** Causes and effects of food adulteration
**Topic sentence:** Food adulteration is a growing problem in Bangladesh that has clear causes and dangerous effects on public health.
**Supporting point 1:** Cause: greed for quick profit
**Supporting point 2:** Cause: weak monitoring and punishment
**Supporting point 3:** Effects: serious illness and loss of trust
**Concluding sentence:** Therefore, greed and weak enforcement cause food adulteration, and the nation's health pays the price.

Food adulteration is a growing problem in Bangladesh that has clear causes and dangerous effects on public health. The main cause is some traders' greed for quick profit. To make fruit look ripe and fresh for longer, dishonest sellers use harmful chemicals such as carbide and formalin, and some mix cheap, low-quality ingredients with spices and oil. As a result, they earn more money while selling poorer food. Another cause is weak monitoring and punishment. Markets are not inspected regularly, and those who are caught often pay only a small fine. Consequently, many offenders continue the practice without fear. The effects of adulteration are extremely harmful. Chemicals in food can damage the liver and kidneys and may even cause cancer, and children are especially at risk. Moreover, people begin to lose trust in markets and food sellers, so they worry about everything they buy. Therefore, greed and weak enforcement cause food adulteration, and the nation's health pays the price.

*(168 words.)*
