# Reading Comprehension

## What reading really means

The slides define reading as *the process of looking at written symbols and constructing meaning from them*. Reading is not just saying the words; it is understanding, interpreting and making meaning. Good readers combine three things: the words on the page, their language skills and their own background knowledge.

In the exam, this means one thing: **every answer must be supported by the passage.** Your opinion is only asked for when the question says "in your opinion".

## The three reading strategies

| Strategy | What it means | When to use it in the exam |
|---|---|---|
| **Skimming** | Reading quickly for the main idea or general meaning, without focusing on every detail | "What is the main argument/idea of the passage?" and the summary |
| **Scanning** | Reading quickly to find specific information: a name, date, number or keyword | "How many…", "According to the passage, which…", "In which year…" |
| **Inferencing** | Using the text plus your background knowledge to understand what is not stated directly | "Why do you think…", "What does the title signify…", "What can we infer…" |

The slides' example of an inference: *"Sarah grabbed her umbrella before leaving."* The text does not say it is raining, but you can infer that it is raining or likely to rain.

## The question types in Part A

### 1A. Multiple choice

Choose the best option. The wrong options are usually built in predictable ways:

- **Too extreme:** words like *only*, *always*, *never*, *all* ("Money is the only thing that keeps people working").
- **Opposite:** the reverse of what the passage says.
- **True but not the answer:** a true detail that does not answer this particular question.
- **Not mentioned:** sounds reasonable but the passage never says it.

### 1B. Word meanings in context

You get a box of words and a list of definitions. Match each definition to a word *as it is used in the passage*.

1. Find each word in the passage and read the whole sentence around it.
2. Decide the part of speech the definition needs ("the amount of work..." is a noun; "to make smaller" is a verb).
3. Match the definitions you are sure of first; the last one is then easy.
4. The box may contain extra words. Do not force every word into an answer.

### 1C. Short answers in your own words

Usually three questions worth 1, 1 and 2 marks. The last one often asks for your opinion.

- Answer in **one or two complete sentences** for a 1-mark question, three or four for a 2-mark question.
- Begin by turning the question into a statement: *"Why is a high salary not enough?"* becomes *"A high salary is not enough because..."*.
- **Paraphrase.** Change the words and the sentence structure, but keep the meaning. Copying a full sentence loses marks.
- For opinion questions, give your view, one reason and one example.

### 1D. Summary

Covered in its own lesson, **Summary Writing**.

## Step-by-step method for a passage

1. **Read the questions first** (2 minutes). Now you know what to look for.
2. **Skim the passage** for the main idea. Write a 3 to 5 word note beside each paragraph: *"causes"*, *"effects"*, *"solutions"*.
3. **Answer the multiple choice** by scanning for the keywords of each question. Check the sentence before and after.
4. **Match the words** using the context method above.
5. **Write the short answers**, locating each answer in the passage first, then rewriting it in your own words.
6. **Write the summary** last, using your paragraph notes as the plan.

## Paraphrasing: the core skill

| Technique | Original | Paraphrase |
|---|---|---|
| Synonyms | People of fixed income are suffering a lot. | People with limited earnings are facing great hardship. |
| Change word form | Price hike is hindering development. | The country cannot develop properly because prices keep rising. |
| Change sentence structure | Dishonest businessmen create shortages for higher profits. | To earn more profit, some dishonest traders create artificial shortages. |
| Combine ideas | Many people are poor. They cannot buy medicine. | Many poor people cannot even afford medicine. |

> **Rule of thumb:** if four or more words in a row are the same as the passage, rewrite the sentence.

## Revision summary

- Skim for the main idea, scan for facts, infer for what is implied.
- Every answer must be supported by the passage unless the question asks for your opinion.
- In multiple choice, reject options that are extreme, opposite, true-but-irrelevant or not mentioned.
- In word matching, use the sentence around the word and the part of speech.
- Short answers: complete sentences, your own words, length matched to marks.

## Practice

Read the passage, then answer the questions. It is a new passage written for practice, at the length and level of the midterm.

> **Rooftop Gardens in Crowded Cities**
>
> As cities grow, open land becomes one of the rarest resources. In Dhaka, for example, new buildings replace fields and ponds every year, and many children grow up without ever seeing vegetables growing. Rooftop gardening offers a simple answer to this problem. By turning empty roofs into small gardens, city residents can grow food, cool their homes and reconnect with nature.
>
> The benefits begin with food. A well-planned roof of a few hundred square feet can produce tomatoes, chillies, spinach and gourds for a family throughout the year. Because the vegetables are grown at home, families know that no harmful chemicals have been used. Rooftop gardens also help the environment. Plants absorb heat, so the rooms below a green roof stay noticeably cooler in summer, and families spend less on electricity for fans and air conditioners. In addition, plants absorb rainwater, which reduces the pressure on the city's overloaded drains.
>
> However, rooftop gardening is not without challenges. Soil and water are heavy, so a roof must be strong enough to carry them. Water can leak into the building if the roof is not properly sealed. Many residents also believe that gardening takes too much time and knowledge. Experts say that these problems can be solved by using light containers, waterproof sheets and simple training programmes.
>
> Some city authorities now encourage rooftop gardening by offering a small reduction in property tax to buildings with green roofs. If more people took part, the city could become cooler, greener and healthier. A rooftop garden may look small, but together, thousands of them could change the face of a city.

:::practice Practice 1 · Multiple choice | Choose the best option
i. What is the main idea of the passage?
a) Dhaka has no open land left.
b) Rooftop gardens offer several benefits to city residents despite some challenges.
c) Every building in the city must have a rooftop garden.
d) Rooftop gardens are too expensive for most families.

ii. According to the passage, why do rooms below a green roof stay cooler?
a) Because the roof is painted white.
b) Because plants absorb heat.
c) Because families use more fans.
d) Because water is stored on the roof.

iii. Which of the following is mentioned as a challenge of rooftop gardening?
a) Vegetables do not grow in cities.
b) Plants attract dangerous insects.
c) Water can leak into the building.
d) Neighbours often complain.

iv. What can be inferred about the city authorities mentioned in the last paragraph?
a) They want to stop people from gardening.
b) They believe green roofs are good for the city.
c) They grow vegetables on government buildings.
d) They have made rooftop gardens compulsory.
--- skill
Skimming (i), scanning (ii, iii) and inferencing (iv)
--- hint
For (i), reject the options that are too extreme. For (iv), ask *why* would someone reduce a tax for green roofs?
--- answer
i. **b)** Rooftop gardens offer several benefits to city residents despite some challenges. *(Option a is too extreme; c and d are not stated.)*

ii. **b)** Because plants absorb heat. *(Paragraph 2: "Plants absorb heat, so the rooms below a green roof stay noticeably cooler.")*

iii. **c)** Water can leak into the building. *(Paragraph 3.)*

iv. **b)** They believe green roofs are good for the city. *(Offering a tax reduction shows they want more green roofs; the passage never says gardens are compulsory.)*
:::

:::practice Practice 3 · Short answers | Answer in your own words
i. How do rooftop gardens help families save money? *(1 mark)*
ii. Mention two problems a family may face when starting a rooftop garden. *(1 mark)*
iii. In your opinion, should every new building in a city be required to have a rooftop garden? Give reasons. *(2 marks)*
--- skill
Locating information and paraphrasing; giving a supported opinion
--- hint
For (iii), state your view, give one or two reasons, and refer to an idea from the passage.
--- answer
i. Rooftop gardens keep the rooms underneath cooler, so families need to spend less on electricity for fans and air conditioning. They can also grow their own vegetables instead of buying them.

ii. The roof may not be strong enough to carry heavy soil and water, and water may leak into the building if the roof is not sealed well.

iii. I believe new buildings should be encouraged, but not forced, to have rooftop gardens. A green roof keeps buildings cool and reduces pressure on the drains, which benefits the whole city. However, not every roof is strong enough, and some families have no time to garden. A tax reduction, as the passage suggests, would encourage people without forcing them.

:::practice Practice 4 · Inference | Read between the lines
"Many children grow up without ever seeing vegetables growing." What does this sentence suggest about life in the city?
--- skill
Inferencing: understanding what the writer implies
--- answer
It suggests that city life has lost contact with nature and farming. Because open land has disappeared, children know vegetables only as products in a shop, not as plants. The writer uses this detail to show why rooftop gardens are valuable.
