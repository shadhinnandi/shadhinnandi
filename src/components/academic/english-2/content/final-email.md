# Email Writing

## Purpose

A formal email does the same job as an application, but faster: it asks for something, informs someone or follows up on a matter. At university, students email teachers, the library, the accounts office and club advisers. In the final, an email is the alternative to an application in question 2D (3 marks).

A formal email must be **clear, concise and polite**. Teachers receive many emails a day, so the subject line and the first sentence should tell them exactly why you are writing.

## The reference pattern

The final materials include a handwritten sample email. **Its format is the one to follow.**

## The format, part by part

| Order | Part | What to write | From the sample |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **To** | The recipient's email address | *To: teacher@uiu.ac.bd* |
| 2 | **Subject** | A short phrase stating the purpose | *Subject: Request for extension of Assignment Submission* |
| 3 | **Salutation** | *Dear Sir,* / *Dear Madam,* | *Dear Sir,* |
| 4 | **Opening sentence** | The purpose, immediately | *I am writing to request an extension for submitting my assignment.* |
| 5 | **Details** | The reason, in one or two sentences | *Unfortunately, I have been suffering from illness and could not complete the assignment on time.* |
| 6 | **Request** | What you would like the reader to do | *I, therefore, hope that you would kindly consider my situation and allow me some additional time…* |
| 7 | **Closing** | *Sincerely,* | *Sincerely,* |
| 8 | **Signature** | Name, department, university | *Name / Department of CSE / United International University* |

### How an email differs from an application

| | Application | Email |
|---|---|---|
| Top lines | Date and recipient's postal address | *To:* address and *Subject:* line |
| Subject | *Subject: Application for…* | *Subject: Request for…* (no "Application") |
| Opening | *I am a student of…* | *I am writing to…* (purpose first) |
| Closing | *Yours sincerely,* | *Sincerely,* |
| Signature | Name, ID, department, university | Name, department, university (add ID when the reader needs it) |

## Key phrases

| Purpose | Phrases |
|---|---|
| Opening | *I am writing to request…*, *I am writing to inform you that…*, *I am writing to ask whether…* |
| Giving the reason | *Unfortunately,…*, *Due to…*, *As…* |
| Requesting | *I, therefore, hope that you would kindly…*, *I would be grateful if you could…*, *Could you please…* |
| Closing the body | *Thank you for your time and consideration.*, *I look forward to your reply.* |

## Etiquette

- **One email, one purpose.** Do not mix an extension request with a complaint about marks.
- **Be concise.** Two short paragraphs are enough for most requests.
- **Identify yourself** if the reader may not know you: course code, section, and ID when relevant.
- **Avoid** slang, emojis, text abbreviations (*plz*, *u*, *thx*), all-capital letters and demands (*URGENT!!!*).
- **Never write a vague subject** such as *Hello*, *Help* or *Assignment*.
- **Proofread** before sending: names, dates, course codes and attachments.

## How to adapt the format to a new prompt

1. **Recipient:** use a placeholder address in practice, such as *[course teacher's email address]*. In the exam, a simple address in the style of the sample is acceptable. Do not invent the personal address of a real person.
2. **Subject:** *Request for…*, *Information about…*, *Inquiry about…*
3. **Opening sentence:** *I am writing to…* + the purpose.
4. **Details:** reason and essential facts (dates, course, section).
5. **Request:** *I, therefore, hope that you would kindly…*
6. **Closing and signature.**

## Model emails

Three complete emails for different purposes: a request for a meeting, informing an absence and asking for a reference. The copy button copies the email only.

:::letter Model email 1 · Requesting a meeting | Asking a Teacher for a Meeting about a Midterm Result
**Prompt:** Write an email to your course teacher requesting a meeting to discuss your midterm result.
--- outline
- **To / Subject:** course teacher; request for a meeting to discuss the midterm result
- **Opening:** purpose: a short meeting about the midterm
- **Details:** course and section; lower mark than expected in the writing part; want to understand mistakes
- **Request:** a meeting during office hours this week; thanks
--- answer Email
To: [course teacher's email address]
Subject: Request for a meeting to discuss my midterm result

Dear Madam,
I am writing to request a short meeting with you to discuss my midterm examination result in English II (ENG 1013), Section C. Unfortunately, I received a lower mark than I expected in the writing part, and I would like to understand my mistakes so that I can improve before the final examination.

I, therefore, hope that you would kindly allow me to meet you during your office hours this week at a time convenient for you. Thank you for your time and consideration.

Sincerely,
[Your name]
ID: [Your ID]
Department of CSE
United International University
--- note
The opening sentence states the **purpose and the course** at once. The reason is framed **positively** (*so that I can improve*), not as a complaint about the mark. The writer lets the teacher choose the time, which is polite.
:::

:::letter Model email 2 · Informing and requesting | Informing a Teacher about an Absence from a Presentation
**Prompt:** Write an email to your course teacher informing them that you will miss your group presentation because of a university sports event, and request a new date.
--- outline
- **To / Subject:** course teacher; absence from the presentation and request for a new date
- **Opening:** inform: will miss the presentation on a given date
- **Details:** selected for the university football team; inter-university tournament on the same day; group informed
- **Request:** present on another date; thanks
--- answer Email
To: [course teacher's email address]
Subject: Absence from group presentation and request for a new date

Dear Sir,
I am writing to inform you that I will not be able to attend our group presentation in Principles of Economics (ECO 2101), Section A, on December 4, 2026. I have been selected to represent the university football team in an inter-university tournament, and our match is scheduled for the same day. I have already informed my group members, and they have prepared my part of the slides.

I, therefore, hope that you would kindly allow me to present my part on another date that is convenient for you. I have attached the tournament schedule for your reference. Thank you for your understanding.

Sincerely,
[Your name]
ID: [Your ID]
Department of Economics
United International University
--- note
An email that **informs and requests** gives the information first and the request second. Specific details (course code, section, date) and **evidence** (the attached schedule) make the request easy to approve. The writer shows responsibility by informing the group in advance.
:::

:::letter Model email 3 · Asking a favour | Requesting a Recommendation Letter
**Prompt:** Write an email to a former teacher requesting a recommendation letter for an internship application.
--- outline
- **To / Subject:** former teacher; request for a recommendation letter
- **Opening:** purpose: recommendation letter for an internship
- **Details:** remind the teacher of the course and project; internship name and deadline; offer to send CV
- **Request:** write the letter if possible; thanks
--- answer Email
To: [teacher's email address]
Subject: Request for a recommendation letter for an internship

Dear Sir,
I am writing to ask whether you would be willing to write a recommendation letter for my application to a summer internship at a software company. I took your course Data Structures (CSE 2215) in Spring 2026, where I received an A grade and led the team project on a library management system. The application deadline is January 15, 2027.

I, therefore, hope that you would kindly consider my request. If you agree, I would be happy to send you my CV, my transcript and the internship details to make the process easier for you. Thank you very much for your time and consideration.

Sincerely,
[Your name]
ID: [Your ID]
Department of CSE
United International University
--- note
When asking a favour, the email **asks rather than assumes** (*whether you would be willing*). It **reminds** the teacher who the student is (course, grade, project), gives the **deadline** and offers to **reduce the teacher's work** by sending documents.
:::

## Examination strategy

- **Read the prompt for the recipient, the purpose and the reason.** They fill the *To*, *Subject*, opening and details.
- **Write the frame first:** *To:*, *Subject:*, *Dear Sir/Madam,*, *Sincerely,*, signature.
- **Body:** two short paragraphs (about 70 to 110 words). Purpose and reason in the first; request and thanks in the second.
- **Do not mix formats.** No date and postal address block in an email; no *To:* line in an application.
- **Time:** about 10 minutes.
