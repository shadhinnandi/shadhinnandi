## Part A: Reading (9 marks, CO 2)

Read the passage below and answer the following questions.

> Sleep is one of the most important factors in maintaining good health and well-being. Although many people view sleep as simply a time for rest, it is actually a complex biological process that plays a vital role in physical, mental, and emotional health. For teenagers, who are still growing and developing, getting enough sleep is especially important. Health experts recommend that adolescents get between eight and ten hours of sleep each night to support their overall development.
>
> During sleep, the body carries out many essential functions. Muscles repair themselves, tissues grow, and the immune system strengthens its ability to fight infections. At the same time, the brain remains active. It processes information gathered throughout the day, organizes memories, and prepares the mind for learning new information. This is why students who get adequate sleep often perform better in school than those who do not.
>
> Research has shown that sleep has a direct impact on concentration, creativity, and decision-making skills. A well-rested student is more likely to stay focused during lessons, complete assignments efficiently, and participate actively in classroom discussions. On the other hand, sleep deprivation can reduce attention span and make it difficult to understand or remember information. As a result, students who regularly lack sleep may struggle academically despite their best efforts.
>
> The effects of insufficient sleep are not limited to academic performance. Lack of sleep can also affect emotional well-being. People who do not get enough sleep may become more irritable, stressed, or anxious. Over time, chronic sleep deprivation can contribute to serious health problems, including obesity, high blood pressure, heart disease, and depression. Furthermore, tired individuals are more likely to make mistakes or have accidents because their reaction times are slower.
>
> Modern lifestyles often make it difficult for teenagers to get enough sleep. Many students stay up late studying, using social media, playing video games, or watching online content. The blue light emitted by electronic devices can interfere with the body's natural sleep cycle, making it harder to fall asleep. In addition, irregular sleep schedules can disrupt the body's internal clock and reduce sleep quality.
>
> Fortunately, there are several ways to improve sleep habits. Maintaining a consistent bedtime, limiting screen use before bed, avoiding caffeine late in the day, and creating a quiet and comfortable sleeping environment can all contribute to better sleep. Regular physical activity can also help people fall asleep more easily and enjoy deeper, more restorative sleep.
>
> In conclusion, sleep is not a luxury but a necessity. It supports learning, physical growth, emotional stability, and overall health. By making sleep a priority, students can improve their academic performance, protect their health, and enjoy a higher quality of life.

### 2E(i). Cause and effect essay · Prepared answer

**Outline**
**Topic:** Procrastination Among University Students
**Thesis Statement:** Procrastination among university students is caused mainly by digital distractions and poor time management, and it leads to lower academic performance and increased stress.
**Supporting Points/Plan of Development:** i) Digital distractions ii) Poor time management and fear of failure iii) Effects on academic performance and well-being
**Topic sentence of the first supporting paragraph:** One major cause of procrastination is the constant distraction of smartphones and social media.
**Topic sentence of the second supporting paragraph:** Another cause is poor time management, often combined with a fear of failure.
**Topic sentence of the third supporting paragraph:** Procrastination has serious effects on students' academic performance and well-being.
**Concluding sentence:** Thus, procrastination has clear causes and harmful effects, but students can overcome it with better habits and planning.

**Essay**

"I will start tomorrow" may be the most common sentence in university life. Procrastination, the habit of delaying tasks that should be done now, affects a large number of students. Assignments are started the night before the deadline, and revision begins only days before examinations. Although many students see it as a harmless habit, it has real causes and serious consequences. Procrastination among university students is caused mainly by digital distractions and poor time management, and it leads to lower academic performance and increased stress.

One major cause of procrastination is the constant distraction of smartphones and social media. Many students sit down to study but check their phones after a few minutes. Notifications, short videos and messages offer instant entertainment, while studying feels slow and difficult. As a result, a student who planned to study for two hours may spend most of that time scrolling. Because these platforms are designed to hold attention, it is very easy to lose track of time.

Another cause is poor time management, often combined with a fear of failure. University students must balance classes, assignments, club activities and sometimes part-time work, but many have never learned to plan their time. When a task seems large and unclear, they delay starting it. In addition, some students fear that their work will not be good enough, so they avoid beginning it at all. Consequently, they postpone tasks until the deadline forces them to act.

Procrastination has serious effects on students' academic performance and well-being. Work completed in a rush is usually of lower quality, with careless mistakes and weak research, so grades suffer. Last-minute studying also leads to poor understanding and quick forgetting. Moreover, the pressure of approaching deadlines causes stress, anxiety and lost sleep. Over time, repeated procrastination can damage students' confidence and create a cycle of guilt and further delay.

In conclusion, procrastination among university students is largely caused by digital distractions and weak time management, and its effects include lower grades, stress and poor health. Students can break the habit by switching off notifications while studying, dividing large tasks into small steps and setting personal deadlines. Thus, procrastination has clear causes and harmful effects, but students can overcome it with better habits and planning.

### 2E(ii). Compare and contrast essay · Prepared answer

**Outline**
**Topic:** Working a Part-Time Job vs. Focusing on Academics During University
**Thesis Statement:** Although working a part-time job and focusing only on academics both aim to prepare students for the future, they differ significantly in terms of financial independence, academic performance and skill development.
**Supporting Points/Plan of Development:** i) Similarity: preparing for the future ii) Difference: financial independence and academic performance iii) Difference: skill development
**Topic sentence of the first supporting paragraph:** Both working part-time and focusing on academics are ways of preparing for a successful future.
**Topic sentence of the second supporting paragraph:** However, the two choices differ greatly in their effects on students' finances and academic performance.
**Topic sentence of the third supporting paragraph:** The two paths also develop different kinds of skills.
**Concluding sentence:** In conclusion, both choices have value, and the right balance depends on each student's needs and circumstances.

**Essay**

University years are a time of important decisions, and one of the most common is whether to take a part-time job or devote all one's time to studying. Rising living costs encourage many students in Bangladesh to work as tutors, freelancers or shop assistants, while others prefer to concentrate entirely on their courses. Each choice brings different benefits and challenges. Although working a part-time job and focusing only on academics both aim to prepare students for the future, they differ significantly in terms of financial independence, academic performance and skill development.

Both working part-time and focusing on academics are ways of preparing for a successful future. A student who studies full-time aims to earn excellent grades, gain deep knowledge and qualify for scholarships, higher studies or good jobs. Similarly, a student who works part-time aims to build a career by gaining experience and contacts. In both cases, the student is investing time and effort in long-term goals. Therefore, neither choice is a sign of a lack of ambition; both reflect a desire to succeed.

However, the two choices differ greatly in their effects on students' finances and academic performance. Students with part-time jobs earn their own money, so they can pay part of their fees, support their families and learn to manage a budget. In contrast, students who focus only on studies usually depend on their families. On the other hand, full-time students have more time for lectures, revision and assignments, which often leads to higher grades. Working students, especially those who work long hours, may miss classes, feel tired and fall behind in their coursework.

The two paths also develop different kinds of skills. A part-time job teaches practical skills that classrooms rarely provide, such as communication with customers, teamwork, responsibility and punctuality. These skills make graduates more attractive to employers. Students who focus on academics, by contrast, develop stronger subject knowledge, research ability and analytical thinking, especially if they take part in projects, competitions and academic clubs. Thus, one path builds workplace experience, while the other builds academic depth.

To sum up, working part-time and studying full-time share the same goal of preparing students for the future, but they differ in financial independence, academic results and the skills they develop. A part-time job of limited hours can offer the benefits of both, provided studies remain the priority. In conclusion, both choices have value, and the right balance depends on each student's needs and circumstances.

### 2E(iii). Argumentative essay · Prepared answer

**Outline**
**Topic:** Should Animals be Used for Scientific Research?
**Thesis Statement:** I strongly believe that animals should be used for scientific research, under strict ethical rules, because such research saves human lives, ensures the safety of medicines and also benefits animals themselves.
**Supporting Points/Plan of Development:** i) Saves human lives ii) Ensures the safety of medicines and products iii) Benefits animal health iv) Refutation of the opposing argument
**Topic sentence of the first supporting paragraph:** Firstly, animal research has played a vital role in saving millions of human lives.
**Topic sentence of the second supporting paragraph:** Secondly, testing on animals helps ensure that new medicines are safe before they are given to people.
**Topic sentence of the third supporting paragraph:** Finally, animal research also improves the health of animals.
**Refutation/Counterargument:** Some people argue that animal research is cruel. However, strict laws and modern methods can greatly reduce suffering while preserving the benefits.
**Concluding sentence:** In conclusion, it can be argued that animals should be used for scientific research, provided it is carried out responsibly and humanely.

**Essay**

Almost every medicine in a family's cupboard, from common antibiotics to vaccines, was developed with the help of research on animals. Yet the use of animals in laboratories is one of the most debated issues in modern science. Animal rights groups describe it as cruel, while many scientists consider it essential for medical progress. The question is whether the benefits justify the use of animals. I strongly believe that animals should be used for scientific research, under strict ethical rules, because such research saves human lives, ensures the safety of medicines and also benefits animals themselves.

Firstly, animal research has played a vital role in saving millions of human lives. Many major medical advances, including the development of insulin for diabetes and vaccines against diseases such as polio, depended on studies involving animals. Because animals share many biological systems with humans, researchers can study how diseases develop and test possible treatments. Without this research, many illnesses that are now treatable would still be deadly.

Secondly, testing on animals helps ensure that new medicines are safe before they are given to people. A new drug may have harmful side effects that cannot be predicted from laboratory experiments on cells alone. Testing it on animals first allows scientists to find dangerous effects and determine safe doses. As a result, human volunteers in clinical trials, and later millions of patients, are protected from serious harm.

Finally, animal research also improves the health of animals. Vaccines and treatments developed through research protect pets and livestock from diseases such as rabies and anthrax. Veterinary medicine has advanced greatly because of such studies, and research has even helped protect endangered species from disease. In this way, the same research that benefits humans also reduces animal suffering.

Some people argue that using animals in research is cruel and that animals have the right not to suffer. This concern deserves respect, and unnecessary cruelty can never be justified. However, the answer is strict regulation, not a complete ban. Many countries follow the principle of the "three Rs": replacing animals with other methods wherever possible, reducing the number of animals used and refining procedures to minimise pain. With such rules, the benefits of research can be preserved while suffering is kept to a minimum.

To sum up, animal research saves human lives, protects patients from unsafe medicines and improves the health of animals themselves. Its ethical problems are real, but they can be addressed through strict laws and continued efforts to develop alternatives. In conclusion, it can be argued that animals should be used for scientific research, provided it is carried out responsibly and humanely.
