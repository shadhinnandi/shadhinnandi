The whole midterm syllabus on one page. Each topic comes from the midterm lecture slides; the "exam link" column shows where it earns marks in the paper.

## The topics in detail

### Reading Comprehension

Reading means building meaning from a text, not just saying the words. You will learn three strategies (skimming for the main idea, scanning for a fact, and inferencing for what is implied) and use them on the four question types of Part A. **Why it matters:** reading is the largest single block of midterm marks, and most lost marks come from careless reading rather than difficult English.

### Summary Writing

A summary keeps the main idea and the key supporting points of a passage and drops everything else. You will learn the slides' four-step method: an opening phrase, the key points in logical order, your own words, and a closing phrase. **Why it matters:** the summary is worth 4 marks and reduced marks are given for copying.

### Paragraph Structure

A paragraph is a group of sentences about one idea. You will learn to write a topic sentence with a clear *controlling idea*, to support it with three relevant points and explanations, to connect sentences with transitions and to close with a concluding sentence. **Why it matters:** the exam asks for the outline (topic, topic sentence, three supporting points, concluding sentence) before the paragraph, so structure earns marks directly.

### Descriptive Paragraph

Describes a person, place, object or experience so the reader can see it. You will learn to choose a dominant impression, gather sensory details and order them spatially or by importance. **Why it matters:** descriptive topics appear in almost every paper.

### Narrative Paragraph

Tells a story with a beginning, middle and end in chronological order, usually in the past tense. You will learn to turn an experience into three stages with a clear point. **Why it matters:** narrative topics ask about your own experience, so you can always answer one if you know the pattern.

### Process Paragraph

Explains how to do something or how something happens, step by step. You will learn to group many small actions into three main stages and to signal the order clearly. **Why it matters:** process topics are easy marks if the steps are complete and in order.

### Classification Paragraph

Divides people, things or ideas into groups using one clear basis. You will learn to name the categories in the topic sentence and develop each one with examples. **Why it matters:** classification topics are offered as a choice, and the structure (three categories) maps perfectly onto the three supporting points of the outline.

### Cause and Effect Paragraph

Explains the reasons for something and/or its results. You will learn the slides' pattern: cause, then why or how it happens, then an example, then the effect. **Why it matters:** it trains the reasoning you will need for the cause-and-effect essay in the final.

### Editing and Proofreading

Editing improves grammar, sentence structure, punctuation and word choice. You will learn to recognise each error category from the slides (fragment, run-on, inconsistent tense, irregular verb, subject-verb agreement, missing comma, missing quotation marks, missing article, apostrophe, capitalisation, homonym, pronoun, preposition and spelling) and to write the correction in the exam's format. **Why it matters:** editing is tested in both the midterm and the final.

## Suggested study plan

| Day | Study |
|---|---|
| 1 | Reading Comprehension and Summary Writing |
| 2 | Paragraph Structure, then the Descriptive and Narrative paragraphs |
| 3 | Process, Classification and Cause and Effect paragraphs |
| 4 | Editing and Proofreading; then one full practice paper |
