# Summary Writing

## What a summary is

A summary is a short version of a passage **in your own words**. It keeps the main idea and the most important supporting points, in a logical order, and leaves out examples, repetition and minor details. In the midterm it is worth 4 marks, and the question warns that *copying word for word from the text will result in reduced marks*.

A good summary is:

- **Short:** about one third of the original or less. For a midterm passage, 80 to 120 words is enough.
- **Complete:** it covers every main part of the passage, not just the first paragraph.
- **Objective:** it reports the writer's ideas. It does not add your opinion.
- **Original in wording:** it paraphrases, condenses and combines ideas.

## The method from the slides

The slides give four guidelines.

1. **Read the passage carefully and identify its main idea.**
2. **Begin with a summary phrase:** *This passage discusses…*, *The passage deals with…*, *The passage highlights…*, *The passage focuses on…*, *The passage sheds light on…*
3. **Develop the body by presenting the key points in a logical order.** If the passage discusses a problem, organise it as causes, effects and solutions. Use your own words, and connect ideas with *Moreover*, *Furthermore*, *As a result*, *Consequently*, *Therefore* and *Finally*.
4. **Conclude with a closing phrase:** *Overall,…*, *In conclusion,…*, *Thus,…*, *Therefore,…*, *Ultimately,…*

## Step by step in the exam

1. **Label each paragraph** of the passage with two or three words in the margin (for example, *"definition"*, *"causes"*, *"effects"*, *"solutions"*).
2. **Write one sentence for the whole passage.** This becomes your opening sentence.
3. **Turn each label into one sentence** in your own words. Combine small points into one list ("overpopulation, corruption and smuggling").
4. **Add linking words** so the sentences flow.
5. **Close with one sentence** that restates the overall message.
6. **Check:** no copied sentences, no opinion, no examples or numbers that are not essential.

## Worked example from the slides

The slides summarise the passage *Price Hike in Bangladesh* like this:

> The passage deals with the issue of price hike in Bangladesh, which has become one of the country's major socio-economic problems. It explains that this problem is mainly caused by overpopulation, corruption, business syndicates, smuggling, and the artificial shortage of essential commodities. As a result, many people with limited incomes struggle to meet their basic needs, while some children are forced to leave school. To tackle this problem, the passage suggests increasing food production, ensuring political stability, punishing dishonest businessmen, and maintaining a stable supply of essential commodities. Overall, the passage presents the causes, effects and possible solutions to the problem of price hike.

| Part | What it does |
|---|---|
| *The passage deals with the issue of price hike…* | Opening phrase plus the main idea |
| *It explains that this problem is mainly caused by…* | Causes, combined into one list |
| *As a result, many people…* | Effects, joined with a linking phrase |
| *To tackle this problem, the passage suggests…* | Solutions |
| *Overall, the passage presents…* | Closing phrase |

Notice that the original's emotional sentences ("It is so horrible", "There is no light of hope") are left out. A summary reports ideas, not feelings.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Copying whole sentences | Change words *and* sentence structure; combine ideas |
| Summarising only the first half | Label every paragraph before writing |
| Adding opinion ("I think this is a serious problem") | Report the writer's ideas only |
| Including examples and numbers | Keep only what is essential to the main point |
| Writing a list of disconnected sentences | Use linking words: *Moreover*, *As a result*, *Therefore* |
| Starting with "In this passage I will…" | Use the slides' phrases: *The passage discusses…* |

## Revision summary

> **Formula:** Opening phrase + main idea → key point 1 → key point 2 → key point 3 → closing phrase.
>
> **Checklist:** own words · all paragraphs covered · no opinion · no minor examples · linking words · 80 to 120 words.

## Practice

:::practice Practice 1 · Guided | Choose the best opening sentence
A passage explains why young people are moving from villages to cities, what problems this creates in cities, and how villages could keep their young people. Which is the best opening sentence for its summary?

a) I think migration to cities is a very bad thing for our country.
b) The passage discusses the movement of young people from villages to cities, its consequences and possible remedies.
c) Many young people move to Dhaka every year because they want jobs.
d) In this summary I will write about migration.
--- skill
Identifying the main idea and using a summary opening phrase
--- answer
**b)** It uses a summary phrase and covers all three parts of the passage (causes, consequences, remedies). Option a gives an opinion, c is only one detail, and d announces instead of summarising.
:::

:::practice Practice 2 · Paraphrase | Rewrite in your own words
Paraphrase each sentence without copying more than three words in a row.

i. Because the vegetables are grown at home, families know that no harmful chemicals have been used.
ii. Many residents also believe that gardening takes too much time and knowledge.
--- skill
Paraphrasing by changing words and sentence structure
--- answer
i. Families who grow their own vegetables can be sure the food is free from dangerous chemicals.

ii. Several city dwellers think that keeping a garden demands a lot of time and special skills.

*(Other correct versions are possible. Check that the meaning is the same and the wording is different.)*
:::

Write a summary of the practice passage in the **Reading Comprehension** lesson in about 80 to 100 words.
--- skill
A complete summary: opening phrase, key points in order, own words, closing phrase
--- hint
Label the four paragraphs: *problem and solution*, *benefits*, *challenges and fixes*, *government support and future*. Write one sentence for each.
--- answer
The passage focuses on rooftop gardening as a solution to the shortage of open space in growing cities such as Dhaka. It explains that gardens on roofs allow families to grow safe, chemical-free vegetables, keep their homes cooler, reduce electricity bills and lessen pressure on city drains. However, roofs must be strong and well sealed, and many people think gardening is too demanding. These difficulties, the writer says, can be overcome with light containers, waterproofing and training. Overall, the passage suggests that with official encouragement, rooftop gardens could make cities greener and healthier.
