# Reading: True/False/Not Given and Sentence Making

The final's reading part (9 marks) uses the same strategies as the midterm (skimming, scanning, inferencing) and the same multiple-choice and short-answer tasks. Revise **Reading Comprehension** in the midterm section for those. This lesson teaches the two tasks that are new in the final.

| Task | Marks | Skill |
|---|---|---|
| 1A. Multiple choice | 3 × 0.5 | Scanning and skimming (see the midterm lesson) |
| 1B. **True / False / Not Given** | 3 × 0.5 | Comparing a statement precisely with the passage |
| 1C. **Making sentences** | 4 × 0.5 | Using a word correctly to show its meaning |
| 1D. Short answers | 1 + 1 + 2 | Paraphrasing; supported opinion (see the midterm lesson) |

## True / False / Not Given

You read a statement and decide:

| Answer | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **TRUE** | The statement **agrees with** the passage. |
| **FALSE** | The statement **contradicts** the passage: the passage says the opposite. |
| **NOT GIVEN** | The passage has **no information** on this. You cannot know from the passage whether it is true. |

### The difference between FALSE and NOT GIVEN

This is where most marks are lost. Ask: *Does the passage say something that makes the statement impossible?*

- If **yes**, the answer is **FALSE**.
- If the passage is simply **silent**, the answer is **NOT GIVEN**, even if the statement sounds likely or you know it is true in real life.

Example passage: *"The library opens at 8 a.m. on weekdays. It is closed on Fridays."*

| Statement | Answer | Why |
|---|---|---|
| The library is closed on Fridays. | TRUE | Stated directly. |
| The library opens at 9 a.m. on weekdays. | FALSE | The passage says 8 a.m. |
| The library is open until 10 p.m. | NOT GIVEN | Closing time is never mentioned. |
| The library has a large collection of books. | NOT GIVEN | Probably true, but the passage does not say so. |

### Watch the qualifying words

Words like **always, never, all, only, every, most, some, often, can, may** change meaning completely.

- Passage: *"Students who lack sleep **may** struggle academically."*
- Statement: *"Students who sleep less **always** fail their exams."* → **FALSE**. The passage says *may struggle*, which contradicts *always fail*.

### Step by step

1. Underline the **keywords** of the statement (names, numbers, the main verb, qualifiers).
2. **Scan** for the matching part of the passage. The statements usually follow the order of the passage.
3. Compare **meaning, not words**. The passage will use synonyms.
4. Check the **qualifiers**: *always* versus *often*, *all* versus *some*.
5. Decide: agrees → TRUE; says the opposite → FALSE; silent → NOT GIVEN.
6. Write the full word: **TRUE**, **FALSE** or **NOT GIVEN**, as the instruction asks.

## Making sentences with given words

The instruction is: *make grammatically correct and meaningful sentences with the following words.* Each sentence earns 0.5 marks only if it is **grammatical** and its **meaning shows that you understand the word**.

### Step by step

1. **Identify the word class.** Is it a noun (*obstacle*), a verb (*encourage*), an adjective (*reliable*) or an adverb (*rapidly*)? Use it in that form unless you are sure of another form.
2. **Think of a typical situation** where the word is used.
3. **Add a detail that proves the meaning.** *"This is crucial"* proves nothing; *"Clean water is crucial for good health"* does.
4. **Check the grammar around the word**: articles, prepositions (*aware of*, *responsible for*), singular and plural.
5. **Keep it simple and complete**: one sentence, about 8 to 15 words.

| Word | Weak | Strong |
|---|---|---|
| reliable | My friend is reliable. | My friend is reliable; he always keeps his promises. |
| encourage | I encourage. | My teacher encouraged me to take part in the debate. |
| obstacle | It is an obstacle. | Lack of money was the biggest obstacle to her education. |

The **Vocabulary** lesson has 150 words with model sentences for practice.

## Revision summary

> **T/F/NG:** agrees → TRUE · contradicts → FALSE · silent → NOT GIVEN. Check qualifiers (*always, may, only*). Never use outside knowledge.
>
> **Sentences:** correct word class + typical situation + detail that proves the meaning + correct grammar.

## Practice

The practice uses a new passage written in the style of the final.

> **The Return of the Bicycle**
>
> In many cities around the world, the bicycle is making a comeback. City planners now see cycling as a practical answer to traffic congestion, air pollution and rising fuel costs. Copenhagen, the capital of Denmark, is often described as the world's most bicycle-friendly city, and more than half of its residents cycle to work or school every day.
>
> Cycling offers clear benefits to individuals. It is a form of exercise that fits easily into daily life, and regular cyclists tend to have stronger hearts and lower stress levels. It is also cheap: after the bicycle is bought, the cost of travel is almost zero. For cities, more cyclists mean fewer cars, cleaner air and less noise.
>
> However, cycling can be dangerous where roads are not designed for it. In cities without separate cycle lanes, cyclists must share the road with fast buses and trucks, and accidents are common. Many people also hesitate to cycle because of hot weather or because they have nowhere safe to park their bicycles.
>
> Experts argue that people will cycle if cities make it safe and convenient. Protected cycle lanes, secure parking and public bicycle-sharing schemes have encouraged cycling in many places. If such measures were introduced more widely, the humble bicycle could become one of the most important tools for building healthier cities.

:::practice Practice 1 · True / False / Not Given | Decide for each statement
Write TRUE if the statement agrees with the passage, FALSE if it contradicts the passage, or NOT GIVEN if there is no information on this.

i. More than half of Copenhagen's residents cycle to work or school every day.
ii. Cycling is always safe in every city.
iii. Bicycle-sharing schemes were first introduced in Copenhagen.
iv. Lack of safe parking discourages some people from cycling.
--- skill
Distinguishing contradiction from missing information
--- hint
For (ii), check the qualifier *always*. For (iii), does the passage say *where* bicycle-sharing began?
--- answer
i. **TRUE.** Paragraph 1 says "more than half of its residents cycle to work or school every day."

ii. **FALSE.** Paragraph 3 says cycling "can be dangerous where roads are not designed for it," which contradicts *always safe*.

iii. **NOT GIVEN.** The passage mentions bicycle-sharing schemes but never says where they started.

iv. **TRUE.** Paragraph 3: people hesitate because "they have nowhere safe to park their bicycles."
:::

:::practice Practice 2 · Sentence making | Make sentences
Make grammatically correct and meaningful sentences with the following words.

i. congestion
ii. comeback
iii. secure
iv. practical
--- skill
Showing the meaning of a word in a correct sentence
--- answer
i. Traffic **congestion** near the university makes many students late for morning classes.

ii. After two years of injury, the cricketer made a successful **comeback** in the national team.

iii. Please keep your important documents in a **secure** place.

iv. Carrying an umbrella during the monsoon is a **practical** habit.
:::

:::practice Practice 3 · Short answers | Answer in your own words
i. Why do city planners support cycling? *(1 mark)*
ii. Mention two reasons why some people do not cycle. *(1 mark)*
iii. In your opinion, what should your city do to encourage cycling? *(2 marks)*
--- skill
Locating information, paraphrasing and supporting an opinion
--- answer
i. City planners support cycling because it helps reduce traffic jams, air pollution and the cost of fuel.

ii. Some people do not cycle because roads without separate lanes are dangerous, and because of hot weather or a lack of safe parking.

iii. In my opinion, the city should first build protected cycle lanes on major roads, because safety is the main reason people avoid cycling. It should also provide secure parking at universities, offices and markets. A cheap bicycle-sharing scheme, as the passage suggests, would let students try cycling without buying a bicycle.
:::
