# Introduction to Computer Security

## What is computer security?

**Computer security** is the protection of computer systems, networks and data from unauthorised access, change or damage, so that they keep working as intended.

In simple words: only the right people should **see** the data, only the right people should **change** it, and the system should be **available** when it is needed.

## Why study it?

- Almost every service today (banking, results portals, hospitals, social media) runs on networked computers.
- Attackers look for the weakest point: a bad protocol, a buggy program, a careless user.
- You cannot defend a system if you do not understand how it is attacked.

So the course follows one simple pattern for every topic: **how the attack works, then how to stop it.**

## How the final exam looks

- Total 40 marks, 2 hours, 4 questions with 2 or 3 parts each.
- Most parts are 3 to 5 marks.
- Every paper had at least one **RSA** or **Diffie-Hellman** calculation, and most had a **big modulus** calculation. These are easy marks if you practise the steps.
- Many theory questions are **scenarios**: a short story ("an employee visits a website and...") where you must name the attack, explain how it works and suggest defences.

## How to write a good answer

1. **Name it.** Start with the attack or concept name and a one-line definition.
2. **Explain it.** Give the working steps as numbered points. Add a small diagram if the question asks for one.
3. **Defend it.** List 3 or 4 prevention methods.
4. **Match the marks.** Roughly one clear point per mark.

For calculations, always write the given values first, then the formula, then each step, and box or underline the final answer.
